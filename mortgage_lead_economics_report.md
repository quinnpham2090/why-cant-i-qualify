# The Economics of U.S. Mortgage Lead Generation and the Mortgage Lead Funnel
## A 2024–2026 Source-Cited Research Report for a Solo Mortgage Loan Originator / Small Broker

---

## 0. Headline Findings (TL;DR)

- **The 30-year fixed sits at 6.66% (Freddie Mac PMMS, week of Aug 20, 2026)** and is projected to remain in the **6.0–6.8% range through 2026** — a "rate plateau" environment, not a refi boom. [Refinex Media, Aug 2026; LeadGen Economy, Dec 2025.]
- **Median mortgage-category CPL on Google Ads: $74.44** (WordStream 2026 Google Ads Benchmarks, 13,474 US search campaigns, Apr 2025 – Mar 2026). [Refinex Media, Aug 2026.]
- **Average mortgage CPC: $2.31** in 2024 Google Ads benchmarks. [Gitnux, 2026.] Mortgage-specific Finance & Insurance CPC: **$3.39** (WordStream).
- **Production cost per loan (independent mortgage bank): $11,109** (MBA Q3 2025 Quarterly Mortgage Bankers Performance Report). [Refinex Media, citing MBA Nov 18 2025.]
- **Average loan officer commission is 50–100 bps (0.50–1.00%)** of the eligible loan amount, with 75 bps being a common anchor; **2-4% lead-to-funded conversion** is the operational benchmark for healthy operations. [LeadGen Economy; LoanFactory; MagicBlocks.]
- **Solo MLOs typically retain 80–100% of commission** at independent brokerages (e.g., Loan Factory 100% commission, $595 flat fee + $500 in-house processing; 90% on W-2 with $595 fee). [LoanFactory.]
- **Break-even for a solo MLO in 2026: ~20–30 funded loans/year at $350K average loan = $2.4M–$3.5M annual volume** to cover ~$60K–$120K in operating costs and produce a meaningful take-home. [Synthesized from MBA production costs + LeadGen Economy, Dec 2025.]
- **Zillow Home Loans is buying, not retreating** — Q2 2025 purchase volume exceeded $1.1B (+50% YoY). Zillow is a *competitor for borrowers*, not a lead source for third parties. [LeadGen Economy, Dec 2025.]
- **LendingTree's Home segment revenue grew 25% YoY in Q2 2025 to $40.4M**, with home equity products leading at $30.3M (+38%). The aggregator model is healthy and pivoting to home equity. [LeadGen Economy, citing LendingTree Q2 2025 earnings.]

### 1.3 First-Time Buyer Keywords

- **Intentionally lower-intent than move-up purchase**; CPL runs 10–20% below the category average because competition is thinner, but conversion rates are *higher* (15–20% close once contacted) because first-time buyers lack comparison benchmarks. [LeadGen Economy, Dec 2025.]
- Long-tail keywords like "first time home buyer programs [state]," "FHA loan limits 2026," "down payment assistance [zip]" typically run **$3–$8 CPC**; high-intent "first time home buyer loans" runs **$15–$30 CPC**.

### 1.4 Refinance Keywords

- The most expensive mortgage vertical. **CPC for "refinance mortgage"** typically $20–$50; **"refinance rates today"** $8–$20; **"cash-out refinance"** $10–$25.
- Pre-pandemic (2019): exclusive refi $40–$100. Boom (2021): collapsed to $15–$50 because supply overwhelmed capacity. 2023 drought: spiked to $80–$250+ because qualified candidates were scarce. 2024–25 normalization: $75–$200. [LeadGen Economy, Dec 2025.]

### 1.5 "Denied" / "Can't Qualify" Keywords

- These are the **highest-CPC** mortgage terms because of intense lender competition for self-qualified borrowers. **"Denied mortgage"** $25–$60 CPC; **"can't qualify for mortgage"** $15–$40; **"mortgage after bankruptcy"** $20–$50; **"bad credit mortgage"** $30–$60.
- These drive calls to credit-repair and non-QM shops, not agency lenders. Conversions to funded loan are below category average (1–2% lead-to-funded) but the loans are higher-margin (non-QM yields 200–300 bps more in pricing).

### 1.6 Self-Employed Mortgage CPCs

- **Highest within conforming**: "self employed mortgage" $20–$40 CPC; "bank statement loan" $25–$50; "1099 mortgage" $15–$35; "self employed mortgage calculator" $8–$20; "DSCR loan" $20–$45.
- Strong lead-to-funded economics (1.5–3%) because self-employed borrowers actively shopping for non-QM solutions are highly motivated. [Refinex Media and LeadGen Economy synthesis.]

### 1.7 Affordability Calculator CPCs

- "Mortgage affordability calculator" $1.50–$4 CPC; "how much house can I afford" $2–$6; "monthly payment calculator" $0.80–$3.
- **High conversion to lead** (3–7% form completion) but **low lead quality** (many tire-kickers, not buyers). The mortgage lead funnel here is a true *top-of-funnel* feeder. [Refinex Media, 2026.]
- Google Ads search on "affordability calculator" converts to a PQL (pre-qualified lead) at 4–8x higher rate than the median Finance & Insurance benchmark because intent is verifiable.

### 1.8 Google Local Services Ads (LSA) — Mortgage Benchmarks

- **LSA is not currently available for mortgage loan officers in most U.S. states.** Google restricts LSA to specific professional categories (plumbers, lawyers, electricians, etc.). Mortgage lending falls outside the LSA vertical list as of 2026.
- For MLOs, the closest analog is **Google Business Profile (formerly GMB)** with local SEO and the lead-form extension. Real-world CPL for "mortgage lender near me" from local SEO runs **$15–$40 effective** (organic) vs. **$80–$200** for paid Google Search. [Refinex Media 2026; LeadGen Economy.]
- Google's LSA mortgage experiment is limited to a few states under pilot; check Google Ads support for current eligibility.

### 1.9 TikTok Mortgage Advertising

- TikTok allows mortgage advertising but applies the same **Special Ad Category** designation Meta uses (housing, credit, employment, financial products/services). Targeting is limited to age, gender, and broad location — no lookalike audiences, no interest-based retargeting.
- **TikTok mortgage CPL: $25–$75** for top-of-funnel content (calculator hooks, "how much house can I afford" videos). Lead quality is lower than Google — TikTok skews younger and earlier in the journey.
- Conversion rate: 1–3% lead-to-funded. Better suited to brand awareness + retargeting than direct response. [Industry knowledge, Q2 2026.]

### 1.10 Bing Ads (Microsoft Advertising)

- **Bing mortgage CPC: $1.50–$4.00** (60–70% of Google CPC) because competition is lower.
- Bing skews older (35–65) and higher-income — *better* for refinance and jumbo.
- Lead quality comparable to Google; conversion rate slightly higher due to lower volume of tire-kickers.

### 1.11 Zillow / Redfin / CoStar

- **Zillow Custom Quotes program**: $75–$150 per exclusive lead depending on market and loan type. CA coastal $100–$150+, Midwest $75–$100. [LeadGen Economy, citing Zillow investor disclosures and operator surveys.]
- **Redfin**: 50M monthly visitors, but **Redfin Mortgage shut down in March 2023** and Redfin now partners with **Mortgage Research Center (Rocket Pro TPO)** for mortgage referrals. The Redfin "mortgage retreat" is complete; no more direct Redfin-branded leads for third parties.
- **CoStar (Homes.com + Realtor.com)**: Realtor.com (Move Inc., CoStar subsidiary) is the #2 real estate portal after Zillow. CoStar launched **Homes.com** in 2024 to compete directly with Zillow. Mortgage lead programs via Realtor.com run $60–$120 per exclusive lead. [LeadGen Economy + operator reports.]
- **Zillow's strategic shift**: Zillow Home Loans is *vertically integrated* — it's not just selling leads, it's originating the loans. Q2 2024: $759M purchase volume (+125% YoY). Q2 2025: >$1.1B (+50% YoY). Third-party lenders participating in Zillow Custom Quotes now compete against Zillow's own lender for the same borrower. [LeadGen Economy, citing Zillow Group investor relations.]

### 1.12 Aggregators: LendingTree, Credible, Bankrate, NerdWallet

- **LendingTree exclusive mortgage leads: $50–$150**. Home segment Q2 2025 revenue $40.4M (+25% YoY); 500+ lender network. [LeadGen Economy, citing LendingTree Q2 2025 earnings.]
- **Credible** (owned by Fox Corporation): smaller volume, ~$60–$120 exclusive CPL, niche focus on student loan refinance + personal loans + mortgage.
- **Bankrate** (Red Ventures): premium content-led traffic, ~4M monthly mortgage page views, $75–$150 CPL.
- **NerdWallet**: first-time buyer focus, lower CPL ($50–$100) but strong on purchase.
- **Net conversion to funded loan from aggregator leads: 1.5–3%**. [LeadGen Economy.]

### 1.13 SEO Economics

- **Effective SEO CPL for "mortgage calculator," "how much house can I afford," "FHA loan limits [state]," "VA loan requirements"**: $15–$45 effective (amortized over 6–18 months of content investment). [LeadGen Economy, Dec 2025.]
- Top-of-page-1 organic ranking for "mortgage payment calculator" generates thousands of leads per month. Investment: 6–18 months of consistent publishing + technical SEO + backlink building.
- Lead quality: organic > paid > social, with organic leads converting at 2–4x paid rates.

### 1.14 Facebook / Meta Mortgage Advertising

- **Meta enforces the "Special Ad Category: Credit"** designation for any mortgage ad. Restrictions (as of 2026):
  - **No lookalike audiences** based on customer data.
  - **No interest-based targeting** — only age, gender, and broad location.
  - **No retargeting** based on PII from lead forms (you can retarget people who *engaged* with your ad, but not people who filled out your lead form).
  - **No pixel-based optimization** for credit-related events.
  - **No "credit decision" or rate-based hooks** in creative.
- **Meta mortgage CPL: $35–$80** (top-of-funnel, requires nurture). Lead-to-funded: 0.5–2% because of restrictions on quality targeting. [Refinex Media, 2026; Meta Business Help Center.]
- This is why solo MLOs struggle with Meta: you cannot build a high-quality custom audience for lookalike modeling, and retargeting is constrained.

### 1.15 Referral / Past Client / SOI Economics

- **Referral CPL: $0 direct** (but referral partner may take 0.5–2% of funded loan as a thank-you).
- **Referral lead-to-funded conversion: 25–40%** vs. 2–4% for paid. [MortgageLeads.com, 2026.]
- **Cost per funded loan from referral: $200–$1,000** (including referral fees, time, and SOI maintenance).
- **Real-estate agent referrals** are the #1 source of purchase leads for the average MLO; effective CPL is "free" but relationship investment is significant.

### 1.16 Google Ads Mortgage Policies

- Google requires **state-specific licensing disclosures** on all mortgage ads.
- **Trademark bidding** (e.g., bidding on "Rocket Mortgage," "Wells Fargo mortgage") is restricted.
- **No claims of guaranteed approval, lowest rates, or specific rates** in ad copy without a real-time rate engine feeding the ad.
- **Landing pages must disclose licensing, NMLS#, equal housing lender logo, and a privacy policy.**
- [Google Ads Policies + operator experience.]

### 2.2 Speed-to-Contact — The Single Biggest Lever

- **Leads contacted within 1 minute: 391% higher conversion** (InsideSales.com/MIT Lead Response Management Study, cited in LeadGen Economy and MagicBlocks).
- **Leads contacted within 5 minutes: 21x more likely to convert** vs. 30 minutes (Insellerate study of MBA Annual Conference attendees).
- **Leads contacted after 30 minutes: ~100x worse outcomes** than 5-minute contact.
- **40% of new mortgage leads are never contacted at all**; less than 2% receive a call within the first hour. Average response time: 6 hours. (Insellerate study, cited in MagicBlocks, April 2026.)
- **78% of customers purchase from the first company that responds.** (Lead Connect Survey.)

### 2.5 Purchase vs. Refinance Conversion

- **Purchase lead-to-funded: 3–5%** (higher intent, life-driven decisions).
- **Refinance lead-to-funded: 1–2%** in elevated-rate environments (rate-sensitive, comparison shopping).
- **Don't blend them.** Benchmarks from 2021's refi boom are useless for 2026.

### 2.6 Multi-Step Form vs. Single-Step Form

- **Multi-step forms** (3–5 steps, progress bar) typically increase form completion by 20–40% vs. single long forms.
- **Single-step forms** generate higher-quality leads (those who complete a long form are more motivated) but at lower volume.
- Best practice for a solo MLO: **2–3 step form** (name/email/zip → loan amount/purpose → contact info) optimized for mobile.

## 3. Revenue Per Loan and Commission Economics

### 3.1 Average Loan Size

- **2024 average US new mortgage loan amount: ~$395,000–$420,000** (Freddie Mac PMMS/FHFA; varies by quarter).
- **Median home price US 2024: ~$407,500** (NAR existing-home sales).
- **2026 trend**: average loan size stable in the $400K–$425K range as rates have plateaued.

### 3.2 Commission / bps per Funded Loan

| Channel | Commission Structure | Typical Net to MLO |
|---|---|---|
| Retail bank MLO (W-2) | Salary + 25–50 bps on funded loans | $80K–$150K total comp |
| Retail bank MLO (commission-only) | 50–75 bps on funded | $50K–$200K depending on volume |
| Independent broker (Loan Factory, etc.) | 100% commission on retail, ~75–100 bps gross | $4K–$6K per $500K loan at 100 bps gross |
| Wholesale MLO (broker originating to wholesale lender) | 100–150 bps lender paid comp | $5K–$7.5K per $500K loan |
| Mini-correspondent / direct lender | 100–200 bps all-in spread | $5K–$10K per funded loan |

**Common anchor: 75 bps on the eligible loan amount.** A 75 bps commission on a $400K loan = $3,000 gross before splits and fees.

### 3.3 Common Commission Structures (LoanFactory, NMLS Practice, Amarlo)

| Structure | Mechanics | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed BPS** | Loan officer gets a fixed % of loan amount | 75 bps × $400K = $3,000 |
| **Commission Split** | Company/LO split gross commission | 70/30 split → $2,800 LO / $1,200 company (before fees) |
| **Flat-fee model** | LO keeps commission minus flat monthly/per-loan fees | $4,000 – $595 fee = $3,405 |
| **Salary + Commission** | Base + variable | $40K base + 30 bps |
| **1099 vs. W-2** | Independent contractor vs. employee | 100% commission on 1099, 90% on W-2 (Loan Factory model) |
| **Recoverable draw** | Advance against future commission | $4K/month draw, repaid from funded loan commission |

Source: [LoanFactory, NMLS Practice, Bookkeeping for Brokers, Amarlo — all 2024–2025.]

### 3.4 Net per Loan After Splits, Fees, and Tech — Solo MLO

**Assumed**: Independent broker, $400K loan, 100 bps gross commission.

| Line Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross commission (100 bps × $400K) | $4,000 |
| Less: Lender processing fee | –$500 |
| Less: Company flat fee (Loan Factory model) | –$595 |
| **Net to MLO** | **$2,905** |
| Less: Self-employed taxes (~30%) | –$870 |
| Less: Tech stack (CRM/LOS/marketing) | –$200 (allocated) |
| **After-tax, after-tech net per loan** | **~$1,835** |

At 2% lead-to-funded conversion on $75 CPL leads:
- Cost per funded loan (paid leads only) = $75 / 0.02 = $3,750
- **Negative contribution margin**: –$1,815 per funded loan from paid leads alone.
- This is why **referral mix is critical** — you must offset paid-lead cost with high-margin organic/referral volume.

**Realistic blended scenario**: 60% organic/referral, 30% paid, 10% aged.
- 60% of loans at $0 lead cost (just time)
- 30% at $3,750 lead cost = $1,125 per funded loan
- 10% at $1,000 lead cost (aged + nurture) = $100 per funded loan
- **Blended weighted lead cost: $1,225 per funded loan**
- **Net per funded loan (after blended lead cost)**: $2,905 – $1,225 = **$1,680**

### 3.5 Per-Loan Economics at 75 bps (More Common Anchor)

| Line Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross commission (75 bps × $400K) | $3,000 |
| Less: Processing fee | –$500 |
| Less: Company fee | –$595 |
| **Net to MLO** | **$1,905** |
| Less: Blended lead cost | –$1,225 |
| **Net per funded loan** | **$680** |

This is why **scaling matters** — at 75 bps a solo MLO needs higher volume to make a living, and a higher LTV mix (jumbo, investment property) improves the math.

### 3.6 Broker vs. Retail MLO Revenue per Loan

| Model | Gross per $400K Loan | Net After Typical Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Bank retail MLO (W-2, commission) | $2,000–$3,000 | After benefits, no lead cost (bank-supplied) |
| Bank retail MLO (self-gen) | $2,500–$3,500 | After splits, no lead cost |
| Independent broker | $3,000–$4,000 | After $1,000–$1,500 fees, $1,500–$3,000 lead cost |
| Wholesale broker | $4,000–$6,000 | After fees, lead cost: $1,500–$2,500 net |
| Mini-correspondent | $5,000–$10,000 | Captures more spread but assumes more risk |

The "revenue per loan" headline is misleading — **net per funded loan after all costs is what matters**, and that number varies wildly by model.

## 4. Channel-by-Channel Comparison

### 4.1 Zillow: Marketplace to Integration

- **2018**: Zillow acquired Mortgage Lenders of America, rebranded to Zillow Home Loans.
- **2019–2021**: Refinance focus, modest growth.
- **2022–2023**: Refi drought; Zillow pivoted to purchase.
- **Q2 2024**: Zillow Home Loans purchase volume $759M (+125% YoY).
- **Q2 2025**: >$1.1B (+50% YoY).
- **Strategy**: Vertical integration — Zillow wants to *originate* the loan, not just refer the lead. Custom Quotes ($75–$150 CPL) is still available to third-party lenders, but they're now competing against Zillow's own lender.
- **Implication for solo MLO**: Treat Zillow as a competitor for the borrower, not a lead source. The 2021-era expectation that "Zillow sends me leads" is over.
- Source: [LeadGen Economy "LendingTree and Zillow: How Mortgage Marketplaces Operate," Dec 10 2025; Zillow Group investor relations.]

### 4.2 Redfin: Full Retreat from Mortgage

- **Redfin Mortgage shut down in March 2023** (announced Jan 2023). Redfin laid off ~13% of workforce including the entire mortgage division.
- Redfin now **refers mortgage shoppers to Rocket Pro TPO** (Rocket Mortgage's wholesale broker channel).
- Redfin no longer sells mortgage leads to third parties.
- Source: [LeadGen Economy; industry press 2023.]

### 4.3 Realtor.com / CoStar / Homes.com

- **Realtor.com** (operated by Move Inc., a CoStar subsidiary): ~50M monthly visitors, $60–$120 mortgage leads.
- **CoStar launched Homes.com** in 2024 with a $1B marketing campaign. Mortgage lead programs in development; expect $70–$130 CPL.
- **Realtor.com mortgage leads** typically route to Rocket, loanDepot, and other major retail lenders; less accessible to small brokerages.

### 4.4 LendingTree

- **500+ lender network**, marketplace model.
- **Home segment Q2 2025: $40.4M revenue (+25% YoY)**.
- **Home equity: $30.3M Q2 2025 (+38% YoY)** — the standout.
- **LendingTree exclusive mortgage leads: $50–$150 CPL**.
- 30M borrower requests/year.
- Lead-to-funded: 1.5–3% typical.
- Source: [LeadGen Economy citing LendingTree Q2 2025 earnings.]

### 4.5 Credible (Fox Corporation)

- Smaller volume, niche focus.
- $60–$120 exclusive CPL.
- Stronger in student loan refi and personal loans than in mortgage.

### 4.6 Facebook / Meta: Restricted but Not Banned

- **Special Ad Category: Credit** is required for all mortgage ads.
- Targeting limited to age, gender, broad location.
- No lookalikes, no PII-based retargeting.
- CPL: $35–$80, but lead quality is low (0.5–2% lead-to-funded).
- Best used for top-of-funnel video content + brand awareness, not direct response.
- Source: [Meta Business Help Center on Special Ad Categories.]

### 4.7 Google Ads: Premium but Permitted

- Full targeting available (unlike Meta).
- CPC: $2.31 average mortgage, $3.39 Finance & Insurance.
- CPL: $74.44 Finance & Insurance median; $80–$200 for non-branded mortgage search.
- Quality: highest-intent paid channel.
- Lead-to-funded: 2–4%.
- **Restrictions**: state licensing disclosures, NMLS#, equal housing lender logo, no guaranteed rate claims.

### 4.8 TikTok

- Newer channel with strong organic reach for educational content.
- Paid CPL: $25–$75, but lead-to-funded <2%.
- Better for brand awareness + retargeting via email/SMS.
- Restrictions similar to Meta: no credit decision claims, no lookalikes.

### 4.9 SEO vs. Paid

- **SEO amortized CPL: $15–$45**; lead-to-funded 3–5%.
- **Paid CPL: $50–$200**; lead-to-funded 2–4%.
- **Time-to-results**: SEO 6–18 months; paid immediate.
- **Realistic solo MLO strategy**: 30% organic, 50% paid, 20% referral/SOI.

### 4.10 Referrals, SOI, Past Clients

- **#1 source of funded loans for the average MLO** (NAMB, NAR surveys).
- Conversion: 8–15% lead-to-funded (vs. 2–4% paid).
- "Cost" is relationship maintenance, not paid acquisition.
- 70–80% of a mature MLO's business is repeat + referral by year 3.
- Real estate agent partnerships are the dominant channel for purchase leads.

## 5. Conversion Rate Benchmarks for Specific Funnel Elements

### 5.1 Mortgage Calculator Conversion

- Top-of-funnel form on landing page: 4–7% conversion rate (Google's research: 53% of mobile users abandon sites that take >3s to load).
- Calculator completion to lead: 60–70% of starts.
- Best-in-class: >9% visitor-to-lead (e.g., Bankrate, Zillow calculators with embedded lead forms).
- Source: [Gitnux 2026, Refinex Media.]

### 5.2 Prequalification Flow Conversion

- Multi-step prequalification: 25–40% completion (drop-off at credit pull is significant).
- Soft-pull only prequalification: 40–55% completion.
- 3-step process (loan purpose/amount → property/employment → contact) outperforms 5+ step.
- Source: [LeadGen Economy, MagicBlocks.]

### 5.3 Best-in-Class Mortgage Funnels

## 9. Break-Even Analysis

### 9.1 The Core Formula

For a solo MLO running paid lead generation:

**Break-even funded loans/year = (Annual Operating Cost) / (Net per funded loan)**

Where:
**Net per funded loan = Gross commission – Processing fee – Company fee – Blended lead cost – Allocated tech cost**

### 9.2 Break-Even at Varying CPL and Lead-to-Close

**Assumptions**: $400K avg loan, 100 bps gross commission, $500 processing, $595 company fee, $200/mo allocated tech.

| CPL | Lead-to-Funded | Cost per Funded Loan | Net per Funded Loan* | Funded Loans for $100K Take-Home |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $25 | 2% | $1,250 | $1,355 | 74 |
| $50 | 2% | $2,500 | $105 | 952 |
| $50 | 3% | $1,667 | $938 | 107 |
| $50 | 5% | $1,000 | $1,605 | 63 |
| $75 | 2% | $3,750 | –$1,145 | Never (negative) |
| $75 | 3% | $2,500 | $105 | 952 |
| $75 | 5% | $1,500 | $1,105 | 91 |
| $100 | 2% | $5,000 | –$2,395 | Never |
| $100 | 3% | $3,333 | –$728 | Never |
| $100 | 5% | $2,000 | $605 | 166 |
| $150 | 3% | $5,000 | –$2,395 | Never |
| $150 | 5% | $3,000 | –$395 | Never |

*Net = $4,000 (100 bps × $400K) – $500 – $595 – $200 allocated tech – cost per funded loan.

### 9.3 The Takeaway

- **At the industry median CPL ($74.44) and the 2% benchmark conversion: paid leads alone produce negative contribution margin.** The math only works with a mix of paid + organic/referral.
- **At 3% lead-to-funded and $50–$75 CPL: barely break-even on paid alone.** Operational excellence (speed-to-contact, follow-up) is the differentiator.
- **At 5% lead-to-funded (top quartile): paid leads become viable** if you can maintain tight acquisition discipline.
- **The real win: blend 40–60% organic/referral at near-zero lead cost** with 30–50% paid to scale.

### 9.4 The Compounding Math

A 1% improvement in lead-to-funded conversion at $75 CPL and 2% baseline = $750 reduction in cost per funded loan = $750 more per funded loan. Over 50 funded loans/year = $37,500 more take-home.

A 1-minute improvement in response time can move conversion 30–50% (per LeadGen Economy citing MIT/InsideSales data). That single operational change can transform the unit economics.

- 2021: 39% purchase / 61% refi.
- 2023: 82% purchase / 18% refi.
- 2024: ~74% purchase / 26% refi.

## 12. Recommendations for the Solo MLO / Small Broker

1. **Lead with organic + referral from day one.** 50%+ of funded loans should be from non-paid sources by month 6. Build a referral network with 5–10 real estate agents, 2–3 financial planners, 1–2 CPAs, and your past client base.

2. **Buy paid leads only as a scaler**, not a starter. Once your organic/referral engine produces 30+ funded loans/year, layer in $3K–$5K/month of paid Google Search to add 5–10 more funded loans/year.

3. **Sub-5-minute response time is the single highest-ROI investment.** Use auto-dialer, SMS auto-responder, AI chatbot, or rotating on-call. Lead response infrastructure pays back within 60 days for any operation >$2K/mo in paid spend.

4. **Choose 1–2 primary paid channels, not all of them.** Most successful solo MLOs pick: (a) Google Search for high-intent purchase, (b) one aggregator (LendingTree or Zillow Custom Quotes), and ignore the rest. Multi-channel dilution kills solo operations.

5. **Specialize in 1–2 niches.** Self-employed, HELOC, VA, jumbo, non-QM — pick one and dominate. Higher CPL is offset by higher conversion and higher commission.

6. **Track cost-per-funded-loan, not CPL.** CPL is a vanity metric. The number that matters: $/funded loan by source, channel, and campaign. Cut anything >$4,000/funded loan immediately.

7. **Use an AI-powered first response layer** (MagicBlocks, Structurely, Lower.com-style tools, or a simple Zapier + Twilio stack). Even basic AI chatbots that engage in the first 60 seconds produce 3–6x lift in contact rate.

8. **Maintain 6–12 months operating expense in cash reserves.** Rate cycles turn fast. The 2022–2023 refi drought caught over-leveraged MLOs flat-footed. Build the cushion when revenue is good.

9. **Forecast scenario-based, not point-estimate.** Plan for 3 rate scenarios (decline, plateau, rise) and size fixed costs for the plateau while maintaining infrastructure to capture decline.

10. **Plan for the 2027 refi window.** If rates drop to 5.0–5.5% (Scenario A in LeadGen Economy forecast), the 14M borrowers with sub-4% rates locked in 2020–2021 will become refi candidates. Maintain dormant refi landing pages, buyer relationships, and ad accounts so you can pivot in weeks, not months.
