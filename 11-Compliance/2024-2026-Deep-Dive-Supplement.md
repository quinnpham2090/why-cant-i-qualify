# 1. CFPB RESPA SECTION 8 ENFORCEMENT AGAINST LEAD GENERATORS AND MORTGAGE COMPANIES, 2024–2025

The CFPB's Section 8 (12 U.S.C. § 2607) enforcement against mortgage lead generators and their lender counterparties in 2024–2025 is concentrated in three cases. Two of those cases — Rocket Homes and 1st Alliance Lending — were filed in 2021–2024 and resolved in 2025 by voluntary dismissal. The third — Freedom Mortgage / Realty Connect USA — was filed in 2023 and is the operative precedent for live Section 8 enforcement against lead generators.

## 1.1 Rocket Homes Real Estate LLC; JMG Holding Partners LLC d/b/a The Jason Mitchell Group; 45 real-estate brokerage affiliates; Jason Mitchell (E.D. Mich., No. 2:24-cv-13442)

- **Filed:** Dec. 23, 2024.
- **Dismissed with prejudice:** Feb. 27, 2025 (CFPB notice of voluntary dismissal); court dismissed Feb. 28, 2025.
- **Statute / rule:** RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. § 2607, and Regulation X, 12 C.F.R. Part 1024.
- **Conduct alleged:** The CFPB alleged that Rocket Homes and The Jason Mitchell Group of real-estate brokerages entered into an illegal kickback scheme to steer borrowers to Rocket Mortgage. Rocket Homes was alleged to have provided "things of value" — including payments disguised as advertising and referrals — to the Jason Mitchell Group in exchange for the steering of mortgage-loan referrals. The factual pattern is the classic "MSA-as-disguised-kickback" theory articulated in CFPB Compliance Bulletin 2015-05: a real-estate brokerage in a position to refer settlement service business received payments from a mortgage lender that were, in substance, payment for referrals, not payment for bona fide marketing services actually performed.
- **Resolution:** Voluntary dismissal with prejudice on Feb. 27–28, 2025. No monetary payment; no injunction. (The dismissal followed the change in CFPB leadership in January 2025 and the Acting Director's posture against pursuing the matter.)
- **Significance for diagnostic sites:** The dismissal is procedural, not a substantive finding of no liability. The underlying factual record and CFPB theory of liability (kickbacks via MSAs / lead payments) remain the leading-edge theory. **A diagnostic site that pays a real-estate brokerage for any form of "lead," "co-marketing," or "appointment setting" arrangement is squarely within the conduct the CFPB alleged. The Freedom Mortgage and Realty Connect consent orders (2023) are the live precedent; the Rocket Homes complaint remains the template for the 2024–2025 enforcement theory.**

## 1.2 Freedom Mortgage Corporation and Realty Connect USA Long Island, Inc. (CFPB Nos. 2023-CFPB-0008 and 2023-CFPB-0009, Aug. 17, 2023)

- **Statute / rule:** RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. § 2607; Regulation X, 12 C.F.R. § 1024.14.
- **Conduct found:** Freedom, a residential mortgage loan originator and servicer headquartered in Boca Raton, Florida, provided "things of value" — including subscription services, events, and monthly marketing-services-agreement (MSA) payments — in exchange for referrals of mortgage loans. Realty Connect USA Long Island, Inc., a real-estate brokerage firm based in Suffolk County, New York, accepted those "things of value" from Freedom in exchange for mortgage referrals.
- **Settlement / penalty:** **Freedom — $1.75 million civil money penalty; Realty Connect — $200,000 civil money penalty.** Both required to stop the unlawful activities and comply with ongoing injunctive terms.
- **Direct source:** CFPB enforcement-action page `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/freedom-mortgage-corporation-2023-respa/`; press release "CFPB Penalizes Freedom Mortgage and Realty Connect for Illegal Kickbacks," Aug. 17, 2023 (now archived at the CFPB Public Archive).
- **CFPB public-archive update:** As of 2025 the Freedom and Realty Connect consent orders remain in effect; the CFPB has not moved to vacate them.

## 1.3 1st Alliance Lending, LLC; John Christopher DiIorio; Kevin Robert St. Lawrence; Socrates Aramburu (Conn., No. 3:21-cv-00080; D. Conn., filed Jan. 15, 2021; amended complaint April 1, 2021; stipulated dismissal with prejudice Feb. 28, 2025)

- **Filed:** Jan. 15, 2021 (CFPB); amended April 1, 2021.
- **Stipulated dismissal:** March 13, 2023 (partial); Feb. 28, 2025 (full, with prejudice).
- **Statutes / rules:** Truth in Lending Act (TILA), 15 U.S.C. § 1601 *et seq.*, and Regulation Z, 12 C.F.R. Part 1026; Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 U.S.C. § 1681 *et seq.*; Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA), 15 U.S.C. § 1691 *et seq.*, and Regulation B, 12 C.F.R. Part 1002; **MAP Rule (Regulation N), 12 C.F.R. Part 1014**; and the CFPA's prohibition on unfair and deceptive practices, 12 U.S.C. § 5536.
- **Conduct alleged:** 1st Alliance, based in Hartford, Connecticut, originated residential mortgages from 2004 to September 2019 and stopped operating in November 2019. DiIorio was its CEO; St. Lawrence and Aramburu were the three managing executives. The CFPB alleged violations of TILA, FCRA, ECOA, and the MAP Rule, and that the company and its executives engaged in unfair and deceptive practices under the CFPA.
- **Resolution:** Stipulated dismissal with prejudice on Feb. 28, 2025.
- **Significance for lead generators and qualification sites:** The case is a 2020–2021 enforcement vintage but its MAP Rule allegations are directly relevant to the diagnostic-site context. The CFPB alleged that 1st Alliance made misrepresentations in mortgage advertising — including in lead-generation marketing — that violated the MAP Rule § 1014.3 prohibition on material misrepresentations.
- **Direct source:** CFPB enforcement-action page `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/1st-alliance-lending-llc-et-al/`.

## 1.4 Pattern and Forward-Looking Risk

- The **live Section 8 enforcement precedent** for lead generators in 2024–2025 is the **Freedom Mortgage / Realty Connect** consent order (2023) and the **CFPB v. Rocket Homes** complaint (Dec. 23, 2024). Even after the Rocket Homes voluntary dismissal, the Freedom consent order remains in effect; the CFPB's Bulletin 2015-05 and the Oct. 7, 2020 RESPA Section 8 FAQs remain the operative interpretive guidance.
- **Lead-purchase safe harbor (synthesizing 12 C.F.R. § 1024.14, Bulletin 2015-05, and 2020 FAQs):** (1) no payment keyed to referral volume or value; (2) price reasonably related to the FMV of the lead; (3) no steering / no required use; (4) the website must actually perform a service (collection, validation, deduplication, scrubbing, formatting); (5) no payment to "persons in a position to refer" (real estate agents, brokers, builders, mortgage brokers, attorneys) that "defrays expenses that otherwise would be incurred by" them; (6) no contractual conversion-data feedback loop.
- **For diagnostic sites specifically:** Paying a real estate brokerage to deliver mortgage leads to a single MLO is the Freedom / Realty Connect fact pattern. Even payments framed as "subscription services" or "monthly marketing services" will be scrutinized under § 1024.14(g)(2) for FMV reasonableness and § 1024.14(g)(1)(vi) for the "defray expenses" prohibition.

# 2. INTERCONTINENTAL EXCHANGE (ICE) / ICE MORTGAGE / BLACK KNIGHT ACQUISITION — FTC SETTLEMENT, 2023

## 2.1 Action

- **Case name / docket:** *In re Intercontinental Exchange, Inc., and Black Knight, Inc.*, FTC File No. 221-0058 (consent order May 4, 2023; final order June 13, 2023; FTC allowed acquisition of Black Knight on May 4, 2023).
- **Regulator:** FTC.
- **Underlying transaction:** ICE's $13.1 billion acquisition of Black Knight, Inc. (a major mortgage-technology company providing the Empower loan-origination system, the MSP servicing system, and the Compass Analytics analytics / compliance platform, among others). The CFPB submitted a comment letter to the FTC recommending divestiture.
- **Statute / rule:** Federal Trade Commission Act § 5, 15 U.S.C. § 45 (antitrust) and Clayton Act § 7, 15 U.S.C. § 18.
- **Conduct addressed:** The FTC alleged that the combination of ICE's Encompass loan-origination system with Black Knight's Empower LOS, and ICE's MSP servicing system with Black Knight's MSP servicing system, would substantially lessen competition in the markets for (i) loan-origination systems used by mortgage lenders, (ii) loan-servicing systems used by mortgage servicers, and (iii) the related analytics and compliance offerings (Compass Analytics).
- **Resolution:** ICE agreed to a consent order requiring it to divest **the Empower LOS business** and **the mortgage servicing technology business** (a defined set of assets) to a Commission-approved buyer (ICE divested to a third party; the loan-origination and loan-servicing divestiture closed in 2023). ICE further agreed to non-discrimination and firewall provisions to ensure the divested businesses could compete in the marketplace.
- **Settlement / penalty:** No monetary penalty; behavioral and structural remedies.
- **Relevance to diagnostic / AI mortgage tools:** The FTC's order preserves competition in the mortgage-software stack that powers pre-qualification, origination, and servicing — including the analytics and compliance tools that increasingly use AI. For a diagnostic site, the implication is that (i) the underlying LOS and analytics vendors are not consolidated, preserving pricing leverage for the diagnostic site; (ii) the consent order's non-discrimination provisions affect the data the diagnostic site can purchase from these vendors; and (iii) the FTC's continued interest in mortgage-tech consolidation means that future acquisitions of AI mortgage-tooling companies will be scrutinized.
- **Primary-source URLs:**
  - FTC press release: "FTC Allows Intercontinental Exchange to Acquire Black Knight After ICE Agrees Not to Constrain Mortgage Technology Competition" (May 4, 2023), `https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ftc-allows-intercontinental-exchange-acquire-black-knight-after-ice-agrees-not-constrain-mortgage`
  - Decision and order: Federal Register publication 88 FR 36958 (June 5, 2023).
- **Caveat on the "Compass Analytics" tool:** The prior-session research note in `FAIR-LENDING-AI-MORTGAGE-DIAGNOSTIC-COMPLIANCE.md` describes the Compass Analytics platform as ICE's analytics / compliance offering. Compass Analytics is a mortgage-performance analytics and compliance tool used by mortgage originators, servicers, and investors for HMDA, CRA, fair-lending analytics, and portfolio risk modeling. It is one of the dominant platforms for fair-lending analytics used by lenders to test for disparate impact in their underwriting and pricing. Its combination with ICE's pricing engine raised FTC competitive concerns about pricing analytics in mortgage origination. The divestiture and behavioral remedies address those concerns.

# 3. 2024–2025 REDLINING SETTLEMENTS — CROSSCOUNTRY, CADENCE, FAIRWAY, AND THE DIAGNOSTIC / AI TOOLS COVERED

The three principal 2024 redlining settlements — **CrossCountry Mortgage (October 2024)**, **Cadence Bank (October 2024)**, and **Fairway Independent Mortgage (October 15, 2024)** — are the leading-edge actions in the CFPB / DOJ "Combating Redlining Initiative." They share a common doctrinal framework: (i) ECOA, Regulation B, and the Fair Housing Act (DOJ parallel claim); (ii) CFPA UDAAP; (iii) allegations that the lender failed to serve majority-minority census tracts in its MSA; and (iv) a remedy that combines civil penalty + loan subsidy + community-development investment + branch / office expansion + advertising and outreach.

## 3.1 CrossCountry Mortgage, LLC (D. Minn., Oct. 2024)

- **Case name / docket:** *United States and CFPB v. CrossCountry Mortgage, LLC* (D. Minn., filed October 2024). (The exact docket and case caption are referenced in DOJ and CFPB press releases; the case caption is *United States of America and Consumer Financial Protection Bureau v. CrossCountry Mortgage, LLC*.) The full press release is at the CFPB newsroom archive: `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-and-justice-department-take-action-against-crosscountry-mortgage-for-illegally-redlining-minneapolis-metro-area/` (currently archived; the press release URL was 404 from the CFPB live site in this research session but the action page is the canonical record).
- **Regulators:** CFPB + DOJ (Civil Rights Division; U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Minnesota).
- **Conduct alleged:** CrossCountry avoided providing mortgage services in majority-Black and Hispanic neighborhoods in the Minneapolis–St. Paul MSA, in violation of ECOA, Regulation B, the Fair Housing Act, and the CFPA. The case is one of the principal 2024 actions in the "Combating Redlining Initiative."
- **Settlement / penalty:** **$4.25 million** to a settlement fund (per DOJ press release; precise allocation between civil penalty to CFPB Civil Penalty Fund and loan subsidy is in the consent order). Loan subsidies, community-development investments, branch / office expansion, advertising, and community partnerships are part of the standard remedy.
- **Diagnostic / AI tools covered:** The CrossCountry complaint, like the Fairway and Cadence complaints, focuses on the lender's branch-network and marketing-reach decisions, not on a specific algorithmic decision-tool. The CFPB's theory, however, expressly extends to **the lender's use of digital channels and algorithmic marketing tools that disproportionately exclude majority-minority neighborhoods** — the "digital redlining" theory articulated by the CFPB in its AI-related actions and in its RFI on tenant screening algorithms (discussed below). A diagnostic / qualification site that **only operates online, only advertises in non-minority ZIP codes, or only markets through channels not used by minority consumers** is at risk of a digital redlining challenge even if the site itself does not deny any applicant.
- **Primary-source URL:** CFPB enforcement-action page (verification recommended): `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/crosscountry-mortgage-llc/`

## 3.2 Cadence Bank (S.D. Tex. or comparable federal court, Oct. 2024)

- **Case name / docket:** *United States and CFPB v. Cadence Bank* (suit filed October 2024).
- **Regulators:** CFPB + DOJ (Civil Rights Division; U.S. Attorney's Office).
- **Conduct alleged:** Cadence Bank, a regional bank with mortgage operations, avoided providing mortgage services in majority-Black and Hispanic neighborhoods in the Memphis metro area, in violation of ECOA, Regulation B, the Fair Housing Act, and the CFPA.
- **Settlement / penalty:** Per DOJ press release; the consent order typically combines civil penalty + loan subsidy + community-development investment + branch / office expansion + advertising / outreach. The case is one of the principal 2024 actions in the "Combating Redlining Initiative."
- **Diagnostic / AI tools covered:** Same as CrossCountry — the digital redlining theory applies. Cadence's pattern of marketing-reach and branch-decisions is the focus.
- **Primary-source URL:** CFPB press release: `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-and-justice-department-take-action-against-cadence-bank-for-illegally-redlining-the-memphis-metro-area/` (currently archived at the CFPB Public Archive).

## 3.3 Fairway Independent Mortgage Corporation (N.D. Ala., No. 2:24-cv-01405; filed Oct. 15, 2024; consent order Dec. 3, 2024) — fully detailed in the main report

- **Settlement / penalty:** **$1.9 million civil penalty + $7 million loan subsidy + at least $1 million for new office in majority-Black neighborhood + at least $500,000 advertising and outreach + at least $250,000 consumer financial education + at least $250,000 community partnerships.**
- **Diagnostic / AI tools covered:** The Fairway complaint does not focus on a specific algorithmic decision-tool; the CFPB's theory of liability rests on the lender's branch-location decisions, direct-mail marketing (Fairway "directed less than 3% of its direct mail advertising to consumers in majority-Black areas from 2018–2020"), referral-source network (the "vast majority" of which were in majority-white areas), and the absence of a written marketing or growth plan to address the redlining risk. The Fairway complaint is a fact-pattern template for the "marketing-reach" theory of redlining — not the "algorithmic-decision" theory — and the diagnostic-site takeaway is that **the marketing-reach decisions are independently a basis for liability** even without a specific AI decision.
- **CFPB action page:** `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/fairway-independent-mortgage-corporation/`

## 3.4 Trustmark National Bank (W.D. Tenn., 2021; terminated May 21, 2025) and Park National Bank (S.D. Ohio, 2024) and Rocket Mortgage (E.D. Mich., 2023) — additional 2020s redlining cases

- **Trustmark:** Consent order Oct. 22, 2021; **terminated and dismissed with prejudice on May 21, 2025** (joint unopposed motion by CFPB and DOJ filed May 20, 2025). The vacatur was the new CFPB leadership's enforcement-prioritization choice, not a finding of no liability.
- **Park National Bank:** DOJ sued Park National in 2024 for redlining the Cincinnati metro area; allegations centered on Park National's avoidance of majority-Black and Hispanic census tracts in its mortgage lending.
- **Rocket Mortgage:** DOJ settlement in 2023 of approximately **$3.5+ million** for redlining claims in the Detroit MSA. (DOJ press release URL pattern: `https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-mortgage-pay-375-million-settle-claims-federal-lending-law-violations-and-state`.) Allegations: redlining in the Detroit MSA.
- **Citi (Citibank, N.A.):** Nov. 8, 2023 consent order. **$25.9 million** for intentional, illegal discrimination against Armenian Americans (credit-card-pricing and underwriting). The Citi action is a national-origin ECOA / FHA case, not a redlining case, but it is one of the largest single civil penalty amounts in 2023 fair-lending enforcement. **Citi consent order terminated Oct. 16, 2025** (Citi had paid a $24.5 million civil money penalty and made redress payments before termination).

## 3.5 Digital redlining — the cross-cutting AI / digital theory

- The "digital redlining" theory articulated by the CFPB in the April 2023 Joint Statement on AI and reaffirmed in the August 2022 digital-marketer interpretive rule (CFPB, "CFPB Issues Interpretive Rule on Digital Marketers," August 10, 2022) treats a creditor's digital marketing, advertising targeting, and online service-delivery channels as within the scope of ECOA and the FH Act. The CrossCountry and Cadence actions are the first CFPB / DOJ redlining settlements that build on the digital redlining theory (the theory is referenced in the 2026 Reg B final rule preamble at 91 FR 21620, citing the Truist matter).
- **Implication for diagnostic / qualification sites:** A site that (i) only operates online, (ii) only advertises in non-minority ZIP codes, (iii) only markets through channels not used by minority consumers, or (iv) uses algorithmic targeting to deliver mortgage advertising (e.g., Facebook "lookalike audiences," "Ethnic Affinity" / "Multicultural Affinity" categories) is at risk of a digital-redlining challenge. The site should advertise broadly, ensure equal access across all geographies, document its marketing reach, and maintain a fair-lending monitoring program that includes the digital channel.

# 4. 2025 CFPB GUIDANCE OR ACTIONS ON AI IN MORTGAGE ORIGINATION

## 4.1 The 2025 CFPB guidance withdrawal (90 Fed. Reg. 20,084, May 12, 2025)

- **Citation:** 90 Fed. Reg. 20,084 (May 12, 2025); "Interpretive Rules, Policy Statements, and Advisory Opinions, Withdrawal."
- **Substance:** On May 12, 2025, the CFPB published the withdrawal of **8 policy statements, 7 interpretive rules, 13 advisory opinions, and 39 other guidance documents.** The withdrawal was preceded by the Acting Director's April 11, 2025 internal memorandum prohibiting improper use of guidance by the CFPB and directing a review of all guidance material previously produced.
- **Documents withdrawn include** (the withdrawal is selective; the full list is in the Federal Register entry):
  - 2022 interpretive rule on digital marketers (the "Algorithmic Marketing and Advertising" interpretive rule, August 10, 2022).
  - The 2022 abusive-conduct policy statement (April 3, 2023).
  - The 2022 small-business lending data collection rule withdrawal of compliance dates.
  - 13 advisory opinions across consumer-finance topics.
- **Documents NOT withdrawn** (remain in effect as policy statements under the Administrative Procedure Act):
  - **Circular 2022-03 (May 26, 2022) — Adverse Action Notification Requirements in Connection With Credit Decisions Based on Complex Algorithms** (87 Fed. Reg. 35,864; June 14, 2022).
  - **Circular 2023-03 (Sept. 19, 2023) — Adverse Action Notification Requirements and Proper Use of Sample Forms** (89 Fed. Reg. 27,361; April 17, 2024).
  - The CFPB's two Fair Lending Reports to Congress (FY 2022 and FY 2023).
  - The 2024 AVM Quality Control final rule (jointly issued with the FRB, FDIC, NCUA, OCC; effective on the first day of the calendar quarter following 12 months after publication in the Federal Register).
  - The Feb. 13, 2023 advisory opinion on digital mortgage comparison-shopping platforms (88 FR 9,162).
- **Primary-source URL:** `https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-08710` (search "Interpretive Rules, Policy Statements, and Advisory Opinions, Withdrawal" at `https://www.federalregister.gov/` for the exact citation).

## 4.2 The "Algorithmic Marketing and Advertising" interpretive rule (Aug. 10, 2022) — withdrawn 2025

- **Citation:** 87 Fed. Reg. 48,094 (Aug. 10, 2022).
- **Substance:** Provided that, when digital marketers are involved in the identification or selection of prospective customers for consumer financial products or services, or the selection or placement of content to affect consumer behavior, they are typically service providers under the Consumer Financial Protection Act. When their actions — such as using an algorithm to determine who to market products and services to — violate federal consumer financial protection law, they can be held accountable.
- **Status:** Withdrawn in 90 Fed. Reg. 20,084 (May 12, 2025). **The interpretive rule is no longer in effect; however, the underlying substantive reach of the CFPA over digital marketers is unchanged** — the rule was an interpretation, not a substantive grant of authority. The CFPB's enforcement authority over service providers under 12 U.S.C. § 5481(26) and § 5483(5) is independent of the withdrawn interpretive rule.

## 4.3 The April 25, 2023 Joint Statement on Enforcement of Civil Rights Laws and Consumer Protection in AI

- **Issuing agencies:** CFPB, DOJ (Civil Rights Division), EEOC, FTC.
- **Date:** April 25, 2023.
- **Substance:** The four federal regulators jointly affirmed that they would use their respective authorities (ECOA / Regulation B, the Fair Housing Act, Title VII, FTC Act § 5, and the CFPA's UDAAP authority) to combat discrimination arising from AI and other algorithmic / automated decision-making tools.
- **CFPB enforcement initiatives identified in the Joint Statement:**
  - "Black box" algorithms — the May 2022 Circular 2022-03.
  - "Algorithmic marketing and advertising" — the August 2022 interpretive rule (now withdrawn 2025).
  - "Abusive use of AI technology" — the April 2023 abusive-conduct policy statement (now withdrawn 2025).
  - "Digital redlining" — CFPB's stated priority.
  - "Repeat offenders' use of AI technology" — the proposed Repeat Offender Registry.
- **Status:** The Joint Statement itself is a non-binding statement of enforcement priorities. It was not withdrawn in 90 Fed. Reg. 20,084. The CFPB's enforcement posture as stated in the Joint Statement, however, is in tension with the post-May 2025 CFPB posture; the Joint Statement is the doctrinal anchor that the CFPB's no-action letter to Draper & Kramer (May 15, 2025) effectively walked back.
- **Primary-source URL:** `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-doj-eeoc-and-ftc-joint-statement-on-enforcement-efforts-against-discrimination-and-bias-in-automated-systems/`

## 4.4 The 2024 AVM Quality Control Final Rule (joint CFPB / FRB / FDIC / NCUA / OCC; effective 12 months after Federal Register publication)

- **Citation:** Interagency Final Rule, "Quality Control Standards for Automated Valuation Models" (12 C.F.R. part 323, FRB; 12 C.F.R. part 34, OCC; etc.). The final rule was published in the Federal Register in 2024.
- **Substance:** Requires institutions using AVMs in mortgage origination to have quality-control systems designed to ensure a high level of confidence in the AVM estimate; protect against manipulation of data; seek to avoid conflicts of interest; require random sample testing and reviews; and account for other factors determined to be appropriate. The rule applies to mortgage-origination AVMs.
- **Effect on diagnostic / AI tools:** The rule does not apply to AI tools that are not used in the mortgage-origination valuation process. A pre-qualification diagnostic that does not produce a property valuation is not within the AVM rule. A diagnostic that does produce or rely on an AVM estimate is within the rule, and the lender using the AVM must comply.
- **Status:** In effect (per CFPB press release "CFPB Approves Rule to Ensure Accuracy and Accountability in the Use of AI and Algorithms in Home Appraisals," June 24, 2024, `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/cfpb-approves-rule-to-ensure-accuracy-and-accountability-in-the-use-of-ai-and-algorithms-in-home-appraisals/`).

## 4.5 The 2024 / 2025 AI RFI and the chatbot white paper

- **CFPB AI RFI:** " CFPB Issues RFI on the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Decisionmaking Regarding Consumer Credit and Other Consumer Financial Products and Services" (March 7, 2024). The RFI sought input on the use of AI in consumer-finance decisioning, including underwriting, servicing, and advertising. The comment period closed in 2024.
- **CFPB Chatbots in Consumer Finance report (June 6, 2023):** White paper on the chatbot market and its limitations, and the ways the CFPB is seeing chatbots interfere with consumers' ability to interact with financial institutions. The report identifies the integration of AI chatbots in the consumer-finance industry as a focus area.
- **CFPB Digital Marketer Advisory Opinion (Aug. 10, 2022):** Provided that, when digital marketers are involved in the identification or selection of prospective customers for consumer financial products or services, or the selection or placement of content to affect consumer behavior, they are typically service providers under the Consumer Financial Protection Act. **Withdrawn 2025** (90 Fed. Reg. 20,084).

## 4.6 The April 11, 2025 Acting Director Vought memo and post-May 2025 CFPB posture

- **Substance:** On April 11, 2025, Acting Director Vought issued an internal memorandum prohibiting improper use of guidance by the CFPB and directing a review of all guidance material previously produced. The memo led to the May 12, 2025 withdrawal of guidance documents and to the no-action letter to Draper & Kramer (May 15, 2025).
- **Posture change:** The post-May 2025 CFPB has de-prioritized fair-lending enforcement against mortgage originators that lack "direct evidence of overt racial discrimination and identified victims." HUD, DOJ, state AGs, and private plaintiffs continue to bring actions under the FH Act, ECOA, and state law. **A mortgage diagnostic site cannot rely on the lower CFPB enforcement posture as a complete defense.**

## 4.7 2025 CFPB No-Action Letter to Draper & Kramer (May 15, 2025)

- **Substance:** The Bureau stated: "Draper & Kramer has already paid the civil money penalty and has ceased originating residential mortgage loans so expending resources on monitoring the order is unnecessary. Second, pursuant to the President's 'Restoring Equality of Opportunity and Meritocracy' Executive Order, the Bureau is focusing its supervisory and enforcement resources on fair lending matters with direct evidence of overt racial discrimination and identified victims, which were not present in this matter. Finally, the Bureau is focusing its enforcement and supervision resources on pressing threats to consumers, particularly servicemembers and veterans. Consistent with these priorities, the Bureau will cease monitoring compliance with this order, will not engage in any supervisory activity to assess compliance with the consent order, and will take no steps to enforce the consent order. The Bureau takes this step in the interest of focusing resources on supporting hard-working U.S. taxpayers, servicemembers, veterans, and small businesses."

## 4.8 Summary: 2025 CFPB AI / mortgage posture

- **Circular 2022-03 and Circular 2023-03 remain in effect** and continue to bind creditors. A creditor's use of an AI / ML / "black-box" model is not a defense against ECOA / Reg B adverse-action-notice requirements. The creditor must still provide specific and accurate reasons.
- **The Aug. 10, 2022 digital-marketer interpretive rule and the April 2023 abusive-conduct policy statement are withdrawn.** The underlying CFPA authority over digital marketers and abusive conduct is unchanged.
- **The post-May 2025 CFPB posture de-prioritizes fair-lending enforcement absent direct evidence of overt discrimination.** The Townstone, Trident, and Draper & Kramer vacaturs / terminations reflect this. HUD, DOJ, state AGs, and private plaintiffs continue to bring fair-lending actions.
- **The AVM Quality Control rule is in effect.** A mortgage-origination AVM must have a quality-control system.
- **The June 2024 AI / chatbot symposium and the April 2023 Joint Statement on AI remain the doctrinal anchor** for the proposition that AI is not exempt from federal consumer-finance law. The withdrawal of specific documents does not withdraw the underlying substantive law.

# 5. CFPB INTERPRETIVE RULE ON DIGITAL MORTGAGE COMPARISON-SHOPPING TOOLS (Feb. 7, 2023 press release; 88 Fed. Reg. 9,162 (Feb. 13, 2023) advisory opinion; and the 2024 follow-up)

## 5.1 The Feb. 13, 2023 CFPB Advisory Opinion (88 Fed. Reg. 9,162)

- **Citation:** 88 Fed. Reg. 9,162 (Feb. 13, 2023). CFPB Advisory Opinion, "Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (Regulation X); Digital Mortgage Comparison-Shopping Platforms and Related Payments to Operators."
- **Substance:** The advisory opinion addresses whether payments from mortgage lenders or brokers to operators of digital mortgage comparison-shopping platforms for the routing of consumer inquiries to specific lenders constitute "thing of value" under RESPA Section 8, 12 U.S.C. § 2607. The CFPB's position: payments that vary based on the routing or placement of consumer inquiries are payments for referrals under RESPA Section 8, regardless of how they are characterized.
- **Status:** **Not withdrawn** in 90 Fed. Reg. 20,084 (May 12, 2025). The advisory opinion remains in effect.
- **CFPB press release:** "CFPB Issues Guidance to Protect Mortgage Borrowers from Pay-to-Play Digital Comparison-Shopping Platforms" (Feb. 7, 2023), `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-issues-guidance-to-protect-mortgage-borrowers-from-pay-to-play-digital-comparison-shopping-platforms/`

## 5.2 The Feb. 29, 2024 CFPB follow-up guidance on rigged credit-card comparison-shopping results

- **CFPB press release:** "CFPB Issues Guidance to Rein in Rigged Comparison-Shopping Results for Credit Cards and Other Financial Products" (Feb. 29, 2024), `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-issues-guidance-to-rein-in-rigged-comparison-shopping-results-for-credit-cards-and-other-financial-products/`.
- **Substance:** The CFPB provided guidance on credit-card comparison-shopping tools, warning that the use of dark patterns and manipulated results in comparison tools may violate federal law. The guidance is the credit-card analog to the Feb. 2023 mortgage-platform advisory opinion and addresses the same UDAAP / "kickback" theories.
- **Status:** The Feb. 29, 2024 press release is listed as a CFPB newsroom item; the underlying guidance is an interpretive letter rather than a formal rule. The substantive reach of the CFPA over rigged comparison-shopping tools is unchanged.

## 5.3 The "December 2024 interpretive rule on lead purchases / digital comparison tools"

- **Status of the "December 2024 interpretive rule" claim:** The user prompt references a "December 2024 CFPB interpretive rule on lead purchases" / "December 2024 rule on comparison-shopping tools." **The CFPB's final-rule inventory at `https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/` does not include a December 2024 interpretive rule on lead purchases or comparison-shopping tools.** The CFPB's rulemaking agenda (Reginfo.gov Statement 3170, Spring 2024, Fall 2024, Spring 2025, Fall 2025) does not include a December 2024 RESPA Section 8 / lead-purchase rulemaking. The 2020 RESPA Section 8 FAQs (page last modified March 15, 2024) are the operative CFPB guidance on lead purchases.
- **Possible source of the "December 2024" claim:** The CFPB's *RFI* (Request for Information) on AI in consumer-finance decisioning (March 7, 2024) included a request for input on "lead-purchase" structures, but the RFI is not a rule. The CFPB's Feb. 7, 2023 advisory opinion is sometimes informally described as the "digital comparison-shopping" guidance. The CFPB's Feb. 29, 2024 credit-card follow-up is sometimes described as a "comparison-shopping" guidance.
- **What to tell the user:** **There is no finalized December 2024 CFPB interpretive rule on lead purchases or comparison-shopping tools.** The operative CFPB statements on these topics are the Feb. 7, 2023 advisory opinion (88 Fed. Reg. 9,162), the Feb. 29, 2024 credit-card follow-up, the Oct. 7, 2020 RESPA Section 8 FAQs (page last modified March 15, 2024), and the May 12, 2025 withdrawal of the August 10, 2022 digital-marketer interpretive rule. **The August 10, 2022 digital-marketer interpretive rule has been withdrawn in 90 Fed. Reg. 20,084.**

## 5.4 The "Algorithmic Marketing and Advertising" interpretive rule (Aug. 10, 2022) — withdrawn 2025

- **Citation:** 87 Fed. Reg. 48,094 (Aug. 10, 2022). "CFPB Issues Interpretive Rule on Digital Marketers."
- **Substance:** Provided that, when digital marketers are involved in the identification or selection of prospective customers for consumer financial products or services, or the selection or placement of content to affect consumer behavior, they are typically service providers under the Consumer Financial Protection Act. When their actions — such as using an algorithm to determine who to market products and services to — violate federal consumer financial protection law, they can be held accountable.
- **Status:** **Withdrawn in 90 Fed. Reg. 20,084 (May 12, 2025).**
- **Substantive reach after withdrawal:** The interpretive rule was an interpretation, not a substantive grant of authority. The CFPA's "service provider" definition in 12 U.S.C. § 5481(26) and the supervision / enforcement authority over service providers in 12 U.S.C. § 5483(5) are independent of the interpretive rule and remain in effect.

## 5.5 Practical implications for a mortgage diagnostic / lead-generation site

- **If the site is a "digital comparison-shopping platform" for mortgages** (i.e., it connects consumers to multiple lenders based on a comparison of loan options), the site is within the scope of the Feb. 7, 2023 advisory opinion (88 Fed. Reg. 9,162). Payments from lenders to the platform that vary based on routing or placement of consumer inquiries are payments for referrals under RESPA Section 8.
- **If the site is a single-MLO lead-generation site** (i.e., it captures leads and routes them to one licensed MLO), the site is within the scope of the Oct. 7, 2020 RESPA Section 8 FAQs and CFPB Compliance Bulletin 2015-05. Payments from the MLO to the site that vary based on closed-loan volume are payments for referrals.
- **If the site is a digital advertiser / digital marketing service** for mortgage lenders, the site is within the scope of the (now-withdrawn) Aug. 10, 2022 interpretive rule. The withdrawal of the interpretive rule does not withdraw the underlying CFPA service-provider authority. The site is still subject to the CFPA's UDAAP authority and the FTC Act § 5.
- **If the site uses an AI tool to evaluate the consumer for credit** (i.e., to produce an "approval likelihood" or "pre-qualification" output), the site is within the scope of CFPB Circular 2022-03 and Circular 2023-03 (both remain in effect). If the consumer is denied and the consumer is treated as an "applicant" under Regulation B, the site must provide specific and accurate adverse-action reasons. The withdrawal of the August 2022 digital-marketer interpretive rule does not affect Circulars 2022-03 or 2023-03.
- **If the site advertises using targeted audiences** (Facebook "lookalike audiences," "Ethnic Affinity" / "Multicultural Affinity" categories, etc.), the site is at risk of a digital-redlining challenge even if the site itself does not deny any applicant. See § 3.5 above.
