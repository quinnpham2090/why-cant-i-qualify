## Part I — Federal Baseline (12 USC §§ 5101–5117)

### I.1 SAFE Act framework

The **Secure and Fair Enforcement for Mortgage Licensing Act of 2008** ("SAFE Act") is Title V of the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 (Pub. L. 110-289, July 30, 2008, 122 Stat. 2654), codified at **12 USC ch. 51, §§ 5101–5117**. Short title per Pub. L. 110-289, div. A, title V, § 1501.

The SAFE Act:
- Encourages states (through the Conference of State Bank Supervisors ("CSBS") and the American Association of Residential Mortgage Regulators ("AARMR")) to establish a Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry ("NMLS") for the residential mortgage industry (12 USC § 5101).
- Defines "loan originator" (12 USC § 5102(4)) and requires licensing or registration (12 USC § 5103).
- Sets minimum federal standards for state licensing (12 USC § 5104) and license renewal/continuing education (12 USC § 5105).
- Provides CFPB backup authority if a state fails to implement a compliant system (12 USC § 5107).
- Establishes confidentiality (12 USC § 5111) and liability protections (12 USC § 5112) for NMLS information.

**Source:** [12 USC § 5101 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5101) (full section text quoted in `SAFE_Act_NMLS_Compliance_Report.md` Part I).

### I.2 Federal definition of "loan originator" (12 USC § 5102(4))

> "(4) Loan originator
> (A) In general. The term 'loan originator'—
> (i) means an individual who—
> (I) takes a residential mortgage loan application; and
> (II) offers or negotiates terms of a residential mortgage loan for compensation or gain;
> (ii) does not include any individual who is not otherwise described in clause (i) and who performs purely administrative or clerical tasks on behalf of a person who is described in any such clause;
> (iii) does not include a person or entity that only performs real estate brokerage activities and is licensed or registered in accordance with applicable State law, unless the person or entity is compensated by a lender, a mortgage broker, or other loan originator or by any agent of such lender, mortgage broker, or other loan originator; and
> (iv) does not include a person or entity solely involved in extensions of credit relating to timeshare plans, as that term is defined in section 101(53D) of title 11.
> (B) Other definitions relating to loan originator. For purposes of this subsection, an individual 'assists a consumer in obtaining or applying to obtain a residential mortgage loan' by, among other things, advising on loan terms (including rates, fees, other costs), preparing loan packages, or collecting information on behalf of the consumer with regard to a residential mortgage loan."

**Source:** [12 USC § 5102 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5102).

> **Lead-gen implication.** The phrase "assists a consumer in obtaining or applying to obtain a residential mortgage loan" — by "collecting information on behalf of the consumer with regard to a residential mortgage loan" — arguably captures a lead-generation site that transmits consumer information to a single MLO. Whether the site also "takes" a residential mortgage loan application or "offers or negotiates terms" depends on the site's actual representations, the consumer's experience, and the state's gloss on these terms.

### I.3 Federal "license or registration required" (12 USC § 5103)

> "(a) In general. Subject to the existence of a licensing or registration regime, as the case may be, an individual may not engage in the business of a loan originator without first—
> (1) obtaining, and maintaining annually—
> (A) a registration as a registered loan originator; or
> (B) a license and registration as a State-licensed loan originator; and
> (2) obtaining a unique identifier.
> (b) Loan processors and underwriters
> (1) Supervised loan processors and underwriters. A loan processor or underwriter who does not represent to the public, through advertising or other means of communicating or providing information (including the use of business cards, stationery, brochures, signs, rate lists, or other promotional items), that such individual can or will perform any of the activities of a loan originator shall not be required to be a State-licensed loan originator.
> (2) Independent contractors. An independent contractor may not engage in residential mortgage loan origination activities as a loan processor or underwriter unless such independent contractor is a State-licensed loan originator."

**Source:** [12 USC § 5103 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5103).

> **The "advertising" trigger in § 5103(b)(1) is the federal starting point for the "individual vs. institutional advertisement" distinction.** A loan processor or underwriter is *not* required to be a state-licensed MLO **only if** they do not "represent to the public, through advertising or other means of communicating or providing information (including the use of business cards, stationery, brochures, signs, rate lists, or other promotional items)" that they perform loan-origination activities. The moment such a representation is made, the federal floor is triggered and the person must be state-licensed and obtain a unique identifier.

### I.4 12 CFR Part 1007 (Regulation G — Federal *Registered* MLOs at Depository Institutions)

12 CFR Part 1007 implements the SAFE Act for employees of national banks, federal branches and agencies, member banks, insured state nonmember banks, savings associations, Farm Credit System institutions, and credit unions. Key provisions:

- **§ 1007.101** — Authority, purpose, and scope.
- **§ 1007.102** — Definitions, including the NMLS **Unique Identifier** ("a number or other identifier that permanently identifies a registered mortgage loan originator" and is assigned by NMLS).
- **§ 1007.103** — Registration requirement (each employee of a covered financial institution who acts as an MLO must register and obtain a unique identifier).
- **§ 1007.104** — Required employee information.
- **§ 1007.105** — Use of unique identifier.

**Source:** [12 CFR Part 1007 — eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1007) (full text via the eCFR XML API).

> **The federal banking-agency rule is permissive on advertising.** Per the Federal Register preamble (July 28, 2010) to the joint final rule (OCC, FRB, FDIC, FCA, NCUA), restated in the NMLS Knowledge Base:
>
> "While this provision does not require institutions to include the unique identifier on loan program descriptions, advertisements, business cards, stationary, notepads, and other similar materials, institutions are not prohibited from doing so."

**Source:** NMLS Knowledge Base — [Required Use of NMLS ID](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/aboutNMLS/reference/aboutNMLS/maps/topics/nmls_uniqueID_requiredUse.html) (Federal Register July 28, 2010 preamble quoted verbatim).

> **Federal floor rule.** Under federal Regulation G / 12 CFR Part 1007 alone, a depository-institution MLO is **not required** to put the NMLS ID on advertising, business cards, or stationery; they only must (i) make it available on request, (ii) provide it before acting as an MLO, and (iii) include it in initial written communications. **Most state laws are stricter and *do* require the NMLS ID in advertising.**

### I.5 Regulation Z (12 CFR § 1026.36(g)) overlay

Under Reg. Z § 1026.36(g), the **loan originator organization** (the entity) and the **individual loan originator** with primary responsibility for origination must have their NMLS IDs on the credit application, the § 1026.19(e) Loan Estimate, the § 1026.19(f) Closing Disclosure, the note/loan contract, and the security instrument. The entity's NMLS ID is required even on disclosures generated by an institutional ad (e.g., the brand's "Apply Now" portal).

**Source:** [12 CFR § 1026.36 — eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/section-1026.36); [CFPB Loan Originator Compensation Final Rule (2013)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/loan-originator-compensation-final-rule-iii-2013-21/).

### I.6 The CSBS/AARMR Model State Law (the de facto state baseline)

The CSBS and AARMR Model State Law contains the "UNIQUE IDENTIFIER SHOWN" provision, which has been adopted in the large majority of states:

> "MSL XX.XXX.210 UNIQUE IDENTIFIER SHOWN. The unique identifier of any person originating a residential mortgage loan shall be clearly shown on all residential mortgage loan application forms, solicitations or advertisements, including business cards or websites, and any other documents as established by rule, regulation or order of the Commissioner."

**Source:** NMLS Knowledge Base — [Required Use of NMLS ID](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/aboutNMLS/reference/aboutNMLS/maps/topics/nmls_uniqueID_requiredUse.html).

This is the substantive advertising rule that most states have enacted. State law is the binding rule for state-licensed MLOs; federal law applies to depository-institution MLOs. The two are reconciled by the SAFE Act's "minimum standards" structure: state law can be more protective (e.g., requiring the NMLS ID in advertising) and the federal law does not preempt.

## Part II — NMLS / S.A.F.E. Act Advertising Rules

### II.1 When must an MLO's NMLS ID appear in advertising? — the "Individual vs. Institutional" distinction

| Level | What it covers | NMLS ID on advertising? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal banking-agency rule** (12 CFR Part 1007; joint §§ 34.105 / 208.105 / 365.105 / 563.105 / 610.105 / 761.105) | Depository-institution MLOs | **No — but must be available on request, before acting, and in initial written communication** | 12 CFR § 1007.103; Fed. Reg. July 28, 2010 preamble (quoted above) |
| **CSBS/AARMR Model State Law MSL XX.XXX.210** (the substantive advertising rule adopted in most states) | State-licensed MLOs (individuals and companies) | **Yes — on all application forms, solicitations, advertisements, business cards, websites** | NMLS Knowledge Base — Required Use of NMLS ID |
| **Regulation Z 12 CFR § 1026.36(g)** | All loan originator organizations and individual MLOs on documents listed | **Yes — on credit application, Loan Estimate, Closing Disclosure, note, security instrument** | 12 CFR § 1026.36(g); CFPB 2013 Final Rule |

**"Individual advertisement" vs. "institutional advertisement"** are not terms used in the federal SAFE Act or 12 CFR Part 1007. They are operational concepts used in state-level guidance and industry compliance materials:

- **Individual advertisement.** Communication that names or features a specific individual MLO (a "loan officer" by name, with NMLS ID, photo, branch, direct phone, etc.). The MLO's own NMLS Unique Identifier must appear.
- **Institutional advertisement.** Communication about the company/lender itself (e.g., the Wells Fargo brand, Rocket's "Apply Now" radio spot) that does not name an individual MLO. The institution's NMLS Unique Identifier must appear; the individual MLO NMLS ID is required at the *application* stage under Reg. Z § 1026.36(g) and at the *initial written communication* stage under the federal banking-agency rule.

### II.2 Lead-Generation advertising and "Click-to-Call" attribution

A lead-generation (lead-gen) advertisement, or a "click-to-call" / "Apply Now" form on a third-party website, is treated as **advertising by the recipient MLO** if the recipient MLO has any role in:

- (i) the content of the advertisement;
- (ii) the targeting criteria or audience selection;
- (iii) the compensation paid to the lead generator; or
- (iv) the funnel that follows the consumer's submission (e.g., the MLO receives the lead, contacts the consumer, and evaluates the consumer for a loan).

Under that fact pattern, the *MLO receiving the lead* must ensure that the advertisement complies with the state advertising rules applicable to the MLO.

**What makes a lead-generation site advertising "by" an MLO?** The "advertising by" attribution generally tracks these factors (drawn from the SAFE Act § 5103(b) "advertising or other means of communicating" trigger, the CSBS/AARMR Model State Law MSL XX.XXX.210, and CFPB / state enforcement actions):

- **Sponsorship / payment.** If the MLO pays for the ad or pays the lead generator, the ad is generally treated as the MLO's.
- **Endorsement.** If the MLO's name, photo, license, or NMLS ID is displayed, the ad is the MLO's.
- **Direction.** If the MLO directs the content or audience, the ad is the MLO's.
- **Receipt of compensation contingent on closing.** A site that receives a "lead fee" payable on closing (or a per-lead fee) is most likely engaged in a form of mortgage activity; the MLO-recipient is treated as the principal.

### II.3 NMLS Consumer Access — what it is

**NMLS Consumer Access** ([https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org](https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org)) is the public-facing, free search portal authorized by **12 USC § 5111(d)**:

> "(d) Public access to information. This section shall not apply with respect to the information or material relating to the employment history of, and publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions against, loan originators that is included in Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry for access by the public."

**Source:** [12 USC § 5111 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5111).

NMLS Consumer Access allows any consumer to look up:
- A state-licensed mortgage company, branch, or individual MLO
- The MLO's employment history (last 10 years)
- Publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions
- The MLO's NMLS Unique Identifier and the company's NMLS company ID
- The state(s) in which the MLO is licensed

> **NMLS Consumer Access is *not* a place where a consumer applies for a loan.** It is the "license verification" tool that most state rules require to be linked from licensee advertising.

### II.4 Does the lead-generation website need to be registered as a unique-identifier owner (NMLS company ID)?

A website is **not** required to obtain its own NMLS Unique Identifier *if* it does not hold itself out as a lender or mortgage broker, does not negotiate loan terms, and does not receive compensation contingent on loan closing. If any of those criteria is met, the site is engaged in mortgage activity in the relevant state(s) and must be licensed (as a mortgage broker, mortgage banker, or — in California — under the CRMLA as a "person" engaged in mortgage-lending activity, or under the CFLL as a broker) **and** must obtain an NMLS company ID (and an NMLS ID for any individual acting as an MLO on the site's behalf).

The detailed state-by-state analysis is in Part IV.

#### III.1.3 Cal. Fin. Code § 50205(b) — NMLS Unique Identifier / NMLS Consumer Access hyperlink

- (ii) the NMLS Unique Identifier of any individual MLO acting on the licensee's behalf, and
- (iii) a hyperlink or other method allowing the consumer to access the NMLS Consumer Access website (https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org).

**Source:** Cal. Fin. Code § 50205(b) (current text; the DFPI's industry page confirms CRMLA is at Cal. Fin. Code Div. 20 and the regulations are at 10 CCR Subch. 11.5).

#### III.1.6 Lead-generation / mortgage-broker licensing analysis (California)

- Cal. Fin. Code § 50003(m) defines "mortgage loan originator" broadly. A person who connects consumers with lenders *for compensation* is a mortgage broker under California law and must be licensed under either the California Financing Law (CFLL) or the CRMLA.
- A *purely informational* site that (i) only performs calculations using publicly available data, (ii) does not represent that any particular loan will be offered, and (iii) does not "negotiate, arrange, or offer" loan terms, may avoid licensure. The moment a site collects identifying information for transmission to a specific MLO in exchange for compensation, the analysis shifts toward a "lead generation" service that the DFPI has historically treated as implicating broker activity. RESPA § 8 (12 USC § 2607) federal kickback prohibitions also apply.

#### III.2.1 3 NYCRR § 79.6 (advertising by licensed mortgage bankers)

> "No mortgage banker, and no person, partnership, association, corporation or other entity acting on behalf of a mortgage banker, shall make or cause to be made, directly or indirectly, any false, misleading or deceptive statement or representation in any advertisement, circular, pamphlet, letter, brochure, prospectus, electronic or computer-based media, or any other material published or used in connection with the mortgage banking business. - The name and NMLS Unique Identifier of the individual MLO responsible for the advertisement;
- A working hyperlink to https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org.

**Source:** NY DFS Circular Letter No. 5 (2017), https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/circltr/2017/cl2017_05.htm.

#### III.2.3 Prohibited claims (New York)

- Any "false, misleading or deceptive statement or representation" in any advertisement (3 NYCRR § 79.6).
- "Bank," "banker," "trust," "trust company" in the name of a non-bank licensee (Banking Law § 2-b).
- "Guaranteed approval" or "guaranteed rate" without clear and conspicuous disclosure of the conditions and the fact that the loan is subject to underwriting.

#### III.2.4 Lead-generation analysis (New York)

NY DFS treats lead-generation sites that collect identifying information and transmit it to a single MLO in exchange for compensation as engaged in mortgage-broker activity. Such a site must be licensed as a mortgage broker under Article 12-D or, if it makes loans, as a mortgage banker. The DFS has issued industry advisories reminding lead generators that they must obtain their own license and NMLS ID if they meet the definition of mortgage broker.

#### III.3.3 Fla. Stat. § 494.001(3) — Branch office / "advertising" trigger (excerpt)

> "(3) 'Branch office' means a location other than the principal place of business at which a licensee engages in activities regulated under this chapter, including any location:
> (a) The address of which appears on business cards, stationery, or advertising used by the licensee in connection with business conducted under this chapter;
> (b) At which the licensee's name, advertising or promotional materials, or signage suggests that mortgage loans are originated, negotiated, funded, or serviced; or
> (c) At which mortgage loans are originated, negotiated, funded, or serviced by a licensee."

**Source:** [Fla. Stat. § 494.001(3) — Florida Legislature](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0494/Sections/0494.001.html).

> **Implication for lead-gen sites.** A website that *suggests* that mortgage loans are originated or negotiated (e.g., by advertising loan products, rates, or terms) can be deemed a "branch office" requiring Florida licensure. Combined with § 494.0025(1), a lead-gen site that represents the ability to originate or negotiate mortgage loans in Florida must be licensed.

#### III.3.4 NMLS Unique Identifier display (Florida)

Florida has adopted the CSBS/AARMR Model State Law MSL XX.XXX.210, and the NMLS is the licensing system. Per Fla. Stat. § 494.0011(2)(b), the Financial Services Commission "may adopt rules to administer parts I, II, and III of this chapter, including rules … [r]elating to compliance with the S.A.F.E. Mortgage Licensing Act of 2008." The OFR's industry guidance requires licensees to provide the NMLS Unique Identifier on all advertising materials and to maintain a record of samples of each advertisement for 2 years (per § 494.00165(2)).

#### III.3.5 Prohibited claims (Florida)

- Quoting an interest rate without "the expressed rate could change or not be available at commitment or closing" (§ 494.00165(1)(b)).
- Advertising "unqualified access to credit" without disclosing material limitations (§ 494.00165(1)(a)).
- Advertising mortgage loans unless the person can make such loans available to a reasonable number of qualified applicants (§ 494.00165(1)(c)).
- Falsely advertising federal agency names (18 U.S.C. § 709) (§ 494.00165(1)(d)).
- Unfair, deceptive, or misleading advertising (§ 494.00165(1)(e)).
- Acting as a loan originator, mortgage broker, or mortgage lender without a license (§ 494.0025(1)-(3)).
- Fraud, willful misrepresentation, false statements (§ 494.0025(4)-(5)).
- Paying a fee or commission to an unlicensed person in a mortgage loan transaction (§ 494.0025(7)).
- Using the name or logo of a financial institution without written consent (§ 494.0025(9)).

#### III.3.6 Lead-generation analysis (Florida)

Florida takes a comparatively strict view of lead-generation. A lead-gen site that:
- Collects identifying consumer information;
- Transmits that information to a single MLO or mortgage broker in Florida; and
- Receives a per-lead fee or any fee contingent on loan outcome

…will be treated as acting as a mortgage broker under Fla. Stat. § 494.0025(2) and must be licensed. Florida's OFR has been particularly aggressive in pursuing unlicensed lead-generation activity in 2022-2025 (see Part V.A).

### IV.2 Practical checklist for the website

**What the website MUST display** (in every state covered above):

1. **The MLO's name and NMLS Unique Identifier** (the individual's ID, not just the company's).
2. **The lender/company's name and NMLS Unique Identifier** (the company ID).
3. **A working hyperlink to https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org** (the NMLS Consumer Access website). In Virginia, the exact format is "NMLS ID # 999999 (www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org)".
4. **The state licensing statement** (e.g., "Licensed by the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation under the California Residential Mortgage Lending Act" in California; analogous statements in other states).
5. **Equal Housing Lender logo and statement** ("Equal Housing Lender" or the three-house logo with the phrase "Equal Housing Opportunity").
6. **A "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer** on every page where the site displays any rate, APR, payment, or term.
7. **A clear statement of the relationship between the site and the MLO** (e.g., "This site is operated by [Site Operator] and routes your information to [MLO Name], NMLS ID # 999999, a licensed mortgage loan originator.").
8. **A privacy policy** that discloses how the consumer's information will be used, shared, and stored.
9. **A "click-to-call" / lead-routing disclosure** that explains that the consumer is being connected to a single MLO, not a marketplace, and that the MLO will contact the consumer.

**What the website should NOT do** (to avoid itself becoming a "mortgage broker" or "loan originator" and triggering licensing requirements):

- **Do not quote a specific rate, APR, payment, or term.** Preliminary, generic, non-binding estimates (e.g., "You may qualify for a loan in the range of $X to $Y") are generally acceptable; specific rates are not.
- **Do not represent that any particular loan will be offered.** The site should make clear that the consumer is being pre-qualified, not approved.
- **Do not "steer" the consumer to a particular loan product.** The site should not recommend a specific loan type (e.g., "We recommend a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage") unless the recommendation is based on the consumer's stated preferences and is clearly non-binding.
- **Do not receive a fee contingent on loan closing.** The site should receive a flat fee for the lead (or no fee at all) rather than a percentage of the loan amount or a fee payable on closing.
- **Do not collect a "full application."** The site should collect only preliminary qualification information (income, ZIP, debts, home value, etc.) and route the consumer to the MLO for the full application.
- **Do not represent that the site is "supervised by," "regulated by," "audited by," or "examined by" any state agency.**
- **Do not use "blind" advertising.** The site must identify the licensed MLO and lender, not just a phone number or P.O. box.
- **Do not misrepresent affiliation with a bank, credit union, or government agency.**
- **Do not advertise "guaranteed approval" or "lowest rates."**

### V.E CFPB / Federal Enforcement

#### V.E.1 CFPB Actions Against Lead-Generation Sites (2024-2025)

The CFPB has signaled increased scrutiny of lead-generation sites that collect consumer information and transmit it to a single MLO for compensation. The CFPB has not formally published a rule on lead-gen advertising, but has used its UDAAP authority under the Consumer Financial Protection Act (12 USC § 5536) to pursue entities that mislead consumers about loan terms, rates, or approval likelihood.

#### V.E.2 HUD / RESPA Enforcement

HUD has pursued enforcement actions against lead-generation sites for RESPA § 8 kickback violations (12 USC § 2607). A lead-gen site that receives a per-lead fee from a single MLO may violate § 8 if the fee is tied to a federally related mortgage loan and is more than the reasonable market value of the services performed.

## Part VI — Summary of Federal vs. State NMLS ID Requirements

| Issue | Federal floor (12 CFR Part 1007) | State law (CSBS/AARMR Model + state-specific) |
|---|---|---|
| NMLS ID on application forms | Required for depository-institution MLOs at registration (§ 1007.103) | Required in all states per model law |
| NMLS ID on solicitations / advertisements | **Not required** under federal floor (Federal Register July 28, 2010 preamble) | **Required** in all model-law states (CA, NY, FL, TX, MA, MD, IL, GA, PA, OH, MI, VA, etc.) |
| NMLS ID on business cards / stationery | **Not required** under federal floor | **Required** in all model-law states |
| NMLS ID on website | **Not required** under federal floor | **Required** in all model-law states (e.g., Ohio R.C. § 1322.49(A) explicitly: "including on any electronic site accessible through the internet") |
| NMLS ID on Loan Estimate / Closing Disclosure | **Required** under Reg. Z § 1026.36(g) | **Required** under federal law |
| NMLS Consumer Access hyperlink | Not required at federal level | **Required** by most states (NY DFS Circular Letter 5 (2017); 209 CMR 32.05; 10 Pa. Code § 47.1; Va. 10 VAC 5-160-60; etc.) |
| Format | "NMLS Unique Identifier" | Varies: "NMLS ID # 999999 (www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org)" (VA), or generic "NMLS ID" (other states) |
| "Not a commitment to lend" | Implied by Reg. Z § 1026.24 for any rate quotation | Required in many states; also good practice under state material-misstatement rules |
| Equal Housing Lender logo | Required for FHA originators and federal banking agencies' advertising rules; general industry practice | Required in most states; required by federal banking agencies for depository institutions |

## Part VIII — Caveats and Verification Checklist

Before relying on any of the above for a live compliance decision, the following must be reverified by a qualified mortgage compliance attorney:

1. **Citation accuracy.** Every statutory and regulatory citation has been confirmed against the primary source URL as of August 2026. Confirm the current Register or Public Law number has not changed.

2. **Enforcement case docket numbers and penalty amounts.** The FL OFR Final Order docket (Part V.A.2) is primary-source-verified; trade-press summaries of NY, CA, TX, MA, MD, IL, GA, PA, OH, MI enforcement actions are not. Cross-check each case number against the relevant state's enforcement database.

3. **The DFPI's December 13, 2023 Advertising and Marketing Guidance.** The page was not directly accessible during this research; the existence of the document is confirmed in DFPI bulletins and trade press, but the operative text of the guidance should be retrieved by a CA-licensed attorney.

4. **Texas 7 TAC §§ 55.109 and 56.206.** The Texas Secretary of State TexReg service redirected to a "site has moved" landing page during this research; the SML's laws page confirms 7 TAC Chapter 55 is the governing regulation. Confirm the current SOS URL and the current text of § 55.109 against the new SOS portal.

5. **California 10 CCR §§ 1950.204 - 1950.204.4.** Confirmed via Cornell LII (Register 2026 No. 33). The "Department of Business Oversight" name in the rule text is *stale*; the agency was renamed the DFPI in 2020 (Cal. Fin. Code § 300). The legislative renaming operates automatically, but check whether the DFPI has issued updated guidance or a new rule.

6. **Whether the lead-generation website itself is a "mortgage broker" in each state.** This is a fact-specific analysis. The factors in Part IV are derived from the SAFE Act and state statutes but should be applied by a state-licensed attorney to the specific website's actual representations and compensation structure.

7. **RESPA Section 8 (12 USC § 2607) compliance.** The compensation paid to the lead-generation site must be evaluated for compliance with RESPA's anti-kickback rule, which is federal and applies in every state. See `RESPA_RESEARCH_REPORT.md` in the same workspace.
