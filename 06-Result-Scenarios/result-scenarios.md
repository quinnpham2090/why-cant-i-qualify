1. **Issue identification** — what's the concern
2. **Why it matters** — what lenders think
3. **What could improve it** — potential next steps (not guarantees)
4. **What to avoid** — common mistakes
5. **What an LO would need** — documents and info

All language must be **educational and preliminary** — no approvals, no denials, no guarantees.

## SCENARIO B — High Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)

### Profile
- W-2 employee
- $75k income
- 700 credit
- $2,200/month debt (auto + student loans + credit cards)
- 5% down available
- $300k target price

### Output

> **ESTIMATED COMFORTABLE PURCHASE RANGE: $220,000 – $280,000**
>
> Your target of $300,000 is above the comfortable range based on the income and debt you provided.

### Estimated DTI
- **Back-end DTI: ~58%** (PITI + $2,200 monthly debts vs. $6,250 gross monthly income)
- Most conventional programs cap at 36-45% (FHA may allow up to 56.9% with strong compensating factors)

### Pillar scores
- Income: 🟡 Workable
- Debt: 🔴 Likely Obstacle
- Credit: 🟡 Workable
- Cash: 🟠 Tight
- Payment: 🔴 Likely Obstacle
- Property: 🟢 Strong
- Documentation: 🟢 Strong

### Primary potential obstacle
**Debt-to-income ratio**

> Your total monthly debts (excluding a future mortgage) are roughly $2,200. When an estimated mortgage payment is added, your total monthly obligations may exceed the typical DTI limits for most loan programs.

### Why it matters
Most loan programs look at two ratios:
- **Front-end** (housing only): typically 28-31%
- **Back-end** (housing + other debts): typically 36-43% (FHA up to 56.9% with compensating factors)

Higher DTI = higher risk = harder to qualify.

### Potential next steps
1. **Pay down high-balance credit cards** (often the fastest DTI improvement)
2. **Avoid taking on new debt** (no new cars, no new cards)
3. **Consider a lower target price** to reduce the mortgage payment
4. **Consider FHA** (looser DTI rules if credit and other factors are strong)
5. **Consider a larger down payment** (reduces loan amount, reduces payment)

### What a loan officer would need
- Full credit report to see exact debts
- Minimum payment vs. balance on revolving accounts
- Documentation that student loans are in deferment (if applicable)
- Income documentation
- Co-borrower info (if applicable) to combine DTI

### Recommended next step
**Free 15-minute review.** A licensed professional can run scenarios — including how paying down $X in credit card debt changes the picture, or whether an FHA loan works.

### What to avoid
- Taking on more debt (new car, furniture loans, co-signing)
- Closing credit cards (reduces available credit, can hurt score)
- Maxing out credit cards
- Missing payments (any drop in score matters more with thin DTI)

## SCENARIO E — Self-Employed / Complex Income

### Profile
- Self-employed, 18 months in business (LLC)
- $120k net business income (after expenses)
- 690 credit
- $800/month debt
- 10% down available
- $400k target price
- 2 years personal returns filed

### Output

> **ESTIMATED COMFORTABLE PURCHASE RANGE: $340,000 – $420,000**
>
> Your target of $400,000 is within range, BUT self-employed income documentation is more complex.

### Income note
> Self-employed income is calculated as net business income (after business expenses) averaged over 2 years, with adjustments for depreciation, depletion, and other non-cash items. Your effective qualifying income may differ from what your business "makes."

### Pillar scores
- Income: 🟠 Tight
- Debt: 🟡 Workable
- Credit: 🟡 Workable
- Cash: 🟡 Workable
- Payment: 🟡 Workable
- Property: 🟢 Strong
- Documentation: 🟠 Tight

### Primary potential obstacle
**Self-employed income documentation**

> Self-employed income requires 2 years of personal tax returns, 2 years of business returns, a year-to-date profit and loss, and a business bank statement. With under 2 years in business, traditional loans may be more difficult.

### Why it matters
- Lenders can't use "what you make" — they use tax returns
- Business expenses reduce your qualifying income
- Under 2 years in business is a major hurdle
- Self-employed borrowers often get loans for less than they can afford

### Potential next steps
1. **Wait until 2 full tax years filed** — many traditional loans open up
2. **Bank statement loans** — use 12-24 months of business deposits (higher rates, larger down)
3. **Asset depletion loan** — use assets to "qualify" (higher rates, larger down)
4. **DSCR loan (if investment property)** — based on rental income, not personal income
5. **Co-borrower** with W-2 income can help
6. **Work with an LO experienced in self-employed loans** — much higher approval odds

### What a loan officer would need
- 2 years personal tax returns
- 2 years business returns (if applicable)
- Year-to-date P&L
- Business bank statements
- Business license / articles of organization
- CPA letter (sometimes)
- 2 months personal bank statements

### Recommended next step
**Free 15-minute review with a self-employed mortgage specialist.** Many generalist LOs struggle with self-employed files. A specialist can identify the right program.

### What to avoid
- Taking large distributions right before applying
- Adding significant new business expenses
- Switching business entity type
- Co-mingling personal and business funds
- Missing tax payments

## SCENARIO G — Property / HOA / Condo Issue

### Profile
- W-2 employee, $90k income
- 720 credit
- $400/month debt
- 10% down
- $325k target condo
- HOA $450/month
- Not sure if project is FHA-approved

### Output

> **ESTIMATED COMFORTABLE PURCHASE RANGE: $310,000 – $380,000**
>
> Your target of $325,000 is within range. HOWEVER, condo-specific issues may apply.

### Pillar scores
- Income: 🟢 Strong
- Debt: 🟢 Strong
- Credit: 🟢 Strong
- Cash: 🟡 Workable
- Payment: 🟡 Workable
- Property: 🟠 Tight
- Documentation: 🟢 Strong

### Primary potential obstacle
**Property / HOA**

> Condominiums have additional requirements beyond single-family homes. The HOA must meet lender guidelines, and FHA-insured loans require the project to be on the FHA-approved condo list.

### Specific concerns
1. **FHA condo approval** — if the project isn't approved, FHA loans are unavailable
2. **HOA certification** — many lenders require a HOA questionnaire to be completed (can take 2-4 weeks)
3. **HOA financial health** — high reserves, low delinquency required
4. **Insurance** — HO-6 (walls-in) policy required; master policy must be adequate
5. **Concentration rule** — no single owner can own more than 10% of units
6. **Litigation** — projects in active litigation can be ineligible
7. **Commercial space** — projects with >25% commercial space can be problematic

### Potential next steps
1. **Ask listing agent if the project is FHA-approved** (or in process)
2. **Conventional or VA loans** may be more flexible than FHA for condos
3. **If under contract, request HOA documents early** — don't wait
4. **Consider a different property** (single family) if condo issues are unresolvable
5. **Budget for higher HOA** — affects DTI

### What a loan officer would need
- HOA certification questionnaire
- HOA master insurance policy
- HOA budget and reserve study
- Project legal documents (CC&Rs, bylaws)
- Recent HOA meeting minutes (any pending litigation?)

### Recommended next step
**Free 15-minute review with a condo-savvy licensed professional.** Condo deals can fall apart late in the process if these issues aren't identified early.

### What to avoid
- Falling in love with a condo without verifying project eligibility
- Waiting until under contract to ask about FHA approval
- Skipping HOA document review
- Ignoring HOA financial red flags (low reserves, lawsuits, high delinquencies)
