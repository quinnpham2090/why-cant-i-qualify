# SAFE Act (12 USC 5101 et seq.) and NMLS Advertising Compliance Report
## For a U.S. Consumer Mortgage Qualification Diagnostic Website That Captures Leads and Routes Them to a Single Licensed MLO

> **Scope.** This report is a primary-source research compilation. It quotes the actual federal and state SAFE Act / NMLS / advertising rules, identifies what a lead-generation site must display, and catalogues the major 2022–2026 enforcement themes.
>
> **Citation conventions.** Quoted text is reproduced verbatim from the cited source. Where a section has been renumbered by the issuing agency, both the historical and current citations are given. URLs in brackets are the page from which the text was fetched; the rule URLs are also provided where stable.
>
> **A note on the user's prompt.** Several citations in the request — most notably the "10 CCR Section 2840 series" for California — are **incorrect**. 10 CCR § 2840 (in Title 10, Investment) is administered by the California Bureau of Real Estate (DRE) and concerns *borrower disclosure statements* for real estate licensees, not mortgage advertising by the DFPI. The current location of the California DFPI mortgage advertising rules is **10 CCR §§ 1950.204 - 1950.204.4** in Title 10, Chapter 3, Subchapter 11.5, Article 5 ("Borrowers and Advertising"). The current California NMLS ID / "NMLS Consumer Access" hyperlink requirement lives in **Cal. Fin. Code § 50205(b)**. This report cites the *current* numbers and provides the renumbering context.

## PART I — Federal Baseline

### 1.1 The SAFE Act, 12 USC §§ 5101–5117

#### 1.1.1 Short title and authority (12 USC § 5101)

12 U.S.C. § 5101 provides the federal baseline. It states the purposes of the law and authorizes the creation of the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry (NMLS):

> "In order to increase uniformity, reduce regulatory burden, enhance consumer protection, and reduce fraud, the States, through the Conference of State Bank Supervisors and the American Association of Residential Mortgage Regulators, are hereby encouraged to establish a Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry for the residential mortgage industry that accomplishes all of the following objectives:
> (1) Provides uniform license applications and reporting requirements for State-licensed loan originators.
> (2) Provides a comprehensive licensing and supervisory database.
> (3) Aggregates and improves the flow of information to and between regulators.
> (4) Provides increased accountability and tracking of loan originators.
> (5) Streamlines the licensing process and reduces the regulatory burden.
> (6) Enhances consumer protections and supports anti-fraud measures.
> (7) Provides consumers with easily accessible information, offered at no charge, utilizing electronic media, including the Internet, regarding the employment history of, and publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions against, loan originators.
> (8) Establishes a means by which residential mortgage loan originators would, to the greatest extent possible, be required to act in the best interests of the consumer.
> (9) Facilitates responsible behavior in the subprime mortgage market place and provides comprehensive training and examination requirements related to subprime mortgage lending.
> (10) Facilitates the collection and disbursement of consumer complaints on behalf of State and Federal mortgage regulators."

Source: [12 USC § 5101 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5101) (Pub. L. 110-289, div. A, title V, § 1502, July 30, 2008, 122 Stat. 2810).

Short title: *"Secure and Fair Enforcement for Mortgage Licensing Act of 2008"* or *"S.A.F.E. Mortgage Licensing Act of 2008"*. Codified at 12 U.S.C. ch. 51.

#### 1.1.2 Federal definition of "loan originator" (12 USC § 5102(4))

The federal definition (incorporated into virtually every state implementation) is:

> "(4) Loan originator
> (A) In general. The term 'loan originator'—
> (i) means an individual who—
> (I) takes a residential mortgage loan application; and
> (II) offers or negotiates terms of a residential mortgage loan for compensation or gain;
> (ii) does not include any individual who is not otherwise described in clause (i) and who performs purely administrative or clerical tasks on behalf of a person who is described in any such clause;
> (iii) does not include a person or entity that only performs real estate brokerage activities and is licensed or registered in accordance with applicable State law, unless the person or entity is compensated by a lender, a mortgage broker, or other loan originator or by any agent of such lender, mortgage broker, or other loan originator; and
> (iv) does not include a person or entity solely involved in extensions of credit relating to timeshare plans, as that term is defined in section 101(53D) of title 11.
> (B) Other definitions relating to loan originator. For purposes of this subsection, an individual 'assists a consumer in obtaining or applying to obtain a residential mortgage loan' by, among other things, advising on loan terms (including rates, fees, other costs), preparing loan packages, or collecting information on behalf of the consumer with regard to a residential mortgage loan.
> (C) Administrative or clerical tasks. The term 'administrative or clerical tasks' means the receipt, collection, and distribution of information common for the processing or underwriting of a loan in the mortgage industry and communication with a consumer to obtain information necessary for the processing or underwriting of a residential mortgage loan."

Source: [12 USC § 5102 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5102).

> **Lead-gen implication.** A website that "collects information on behalf of the consumer with regard to a residential mortgage loan" and then transmits that information to a single MLO for compensation arguably *assists the consumer in obtaining a residential mortgage loan*. Whether that conduct rises to the level of "offers or negotiates terms" or "takes a residential mortgage loan application" depends on (i) the website's exact representations about what the consumer will receive, (ii) whether the site receives compensation contingent on a loan closing, and (iii) state-law gloss on the term. See § 1.2 and Part II below.

#### 1.1.3 Federal "license or registration required" (12 USC § 5103)

> "(a) In general. Subject to the existence of a licensing or registration regime, as the case may be, an individual may not engage in the business of a loan originator without first—
> (1) obtaining, and maintaining annually—
> (A) a registration as a registered loan originator; or
> (B) a license and registration as a State-licensed loan originator; and
> (2) obtaining a unique identifier.
> (b) Loan processors and underwriters
> (1) Supervised loan processors and underwriters. A loan processor or underwriter who does not represent to the public, through advertising or other means of communicating or providing information (including the use of business cards, stationery, brochures, signs, rate lists, or other promotional items), that such individual can or will perform any of the activities of a loan originator shall not be required to be a State-licensed loan originator.
> (2) Independent contractors. An independent contractor may not engage in residential mortgage loan origination activities as a loan processor or underwriter unless such independent contractor is a State-licensed loan originator."

Source: [12 USC § 5103 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5103).

> **The "advertising" trigger in § 5103(b)(1) is the federal starting point for the "individual vs. institutional advertisement" distinction.** A loan processor or underwriter is *not* required to be a state-licensed MLO **only if** they do not "represent to the public, through advertising or other means of communicating or providing information (including the use of business cards, stationery, brochures, signs, rate lists, or other promotional items)" that they perform loan-origination activities. The moment such a representation is made, the federal floor is triggered and the person must be state-licensed and obtain a unique identifier. This language is the federal model that the CSBS/AARMR Model State Law (MSL XX.XXX.210) and most state implementations (including Ohio Rev. Code § 1322.49, Fla. Stat. ch. 494, Va. 10 VAC 5-160-60) build on. See § 2 below.

#### 1.1.4 Minimum licensing standards (12 USC § 5104)

Sets federal minimums for background checks, pre-licensing education (20 hours including 3 hours federal law, 3 hours ethics, 2 hours nontraditional mortgage product marketplace), written test (75% passing score), net worth or surety bond, and financial responsibility. State licensing regimes that fall below these minimums trigger CFPB backup authority under 12 USC § 5107.

Source: [12 USC § 5104 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5104).

#### 1.1.5 CFPB backup authority (12 USC § 5107)

If a state does not have in place a licensing/registration system that meets §§ 5104, 5105, and § 5107(d) minimums, or does not participate in NMLS, the CFPB Director "shall provide for the establishment and maintenance of a system for the licensing and registration by the Director of loan originators operating in such State as State-licensed loan originators." The CFPB also "shall coordinate with the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry to establish protocols for assigning a unique identifier to each loan originator licensed by the Director."

Source: [12 USC § 5107 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5107).

> **Practical effect.** Every state has an NMLS-compatible licensing regime, so 12 USC § 5107 backup authority has not been triggered. The relevant state regimes are catalogued in Part II.

#### 1.1.6 NMLS Consumer Access (12 USC § 5111(d))

12 USC § 5111(d) carves out the public-facing portion of NMLS from the otherwise-broad confidentiality protections of § 5111(a)-(c):

> "(d) Public access to information. This section shall not apply with respect to the information or material relating to the employment history of, and publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions against, loan originators that is included in Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry for access by the public."

Source: [12 USC § 5111 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5111).

NMLS Consumer Access (the public search portal at https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org) is the operational implementation of this provision. Many state rules require the licensee to display the NMLS ID and a hyperlink to that site in advertising (see, e.g., Virginia 10 VAC 5-160-60(A)(2) quoted in Part II).

#### 1.1.7 The unique-identifier assignment (12 USC § 5106(b))

The Bureau (CFPB) "shall coordinate with the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry to establish protocols for assigning a unique identifier to each registered loan originator that will facilitate electronic tracking and uniform identification of, and public access to, the employment history of and publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions against loan originators."

Source: [12 USC § 5106 — Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/5106).

### 1.2 Federal Regulation G — 12 CFR Part 1007 (Federal *Registered* MLOs at Depository Institutions)

12 CFR Part 1007 implements the SAFE Act for employees of national banks, federal branches and agencies, member banks, insured state nonmember banks, savings associations, Farm Credit System institutions, and credit unions.

#### 1.2.1 Authority and scope (12 CFR § 1007.101)

> "(a) Authority. This part, known as Regulation G, is issued by the Bureau of Consumer Financial Protection pursuant to the Secure and Fair Enforcement for Mortgage Licensing Act of 2008, title V of the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 (S.A.F.E. Act) (Pub. L. 110-289, 122 Stat. 2654, 12 U.S.C. 5101 et seq.), 12 U.S.C. 5512, 5581, 15 U.S.C. 1604(a), 1639b.
> (b) Purpose. This part implements the S.A.F.E. Act's Federal registration requirement for mortgage loan originators. The S.A.F.E. Act provides that the objectives of this registration include aggregating and improving the flow of information to and between regulators; providing increased accountability and tracking of mortgage loan originators; enhancing consumer protections; supporting anti-fraud measures; and providing consumers with easily accessible information at no charge regarding the employment history of, and publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions against, mortgage loan originators."

Source: [12 CFR § 1007.101 — eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1007/section-1007.101).

#### 1.2.2 Federal "Unique identifier" definition (12 CFR § 1007.102)

> "Unique identifier means a number or other identifier that:
> (1) Permanently identifies a registered mortgage loan originator;
> (2) Is assigned by protocols established by the Nationwide Mortgage Licensing System and Registry and the Bureau to facilitate:
> (i) Electronic tracking of mortgage loan originators; and
> (ii) Uniform identification of, and public access to, the employment history of and the publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions against mortgage loan originators; and
> (3) Must not be used for purposes other than those set forth under the S.A.F.E. Act."

Source: [12 CFR § 1007.102 — eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1007/section-1007.102).

#### 1.2.3 Federal registration requirement (12 CFR § 1007.103)

> "(a) Registration requirement — (1) Employee registration. Each employee of a covered financial institution who acts as a mortgage loan originator must register with the Registry, obtain a unique identifier, and maintain this registration in accordance with the requirements of this part. Any such employee who is not in compliance with the registration and unique identifier requirements set forth in this part is in violation of the S.A.F.E. Act and this part.
> (2) Covered financial institution requirement — (i) In general. A covered financial institution that employs one or more individuals who act as a residential mortgage loan originator must require each such employee to register with the Registry, maintain this registration, and obtain a unique identifier in accordance with the requirements of this part.
> (ii) Prohibition. A covered financial institution must not permit an employee who is subject to the registration requirements of this part to act as a mortgage loan originator for the covered financial institution unless such employee is registered with the Registry pursuant to this part."

Source: [12 CFR § 1007.103 — eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1007/section-1007.103).

> **Federal baseline note.** Subpart 1007 applies to depository-institution employees. State-licensed MLOs (the large majority of the mortgage industry) are governed by 12 CFR Part 1008 (Regulation H) and by state law. The state-licensed regime uses the same NMLS Unique Identifier framework and the same "individual vs. institutional" advertising distinction described in § 2 below.

### 1.3 The Federal Banking Agencies' Use-of-Unique-Identifier Rule

The OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, OTS (legacy), FCA, and NCUA jointly issued a final rule implementing the SAFE Act for depository institutions. The rule text (12 CFR §§ 34.105 (OCC), 208.105 (FRB), 365.105 (FDIC), 563.105 (OTS), 610.105 (FCA), 761.105 (NCUA)) states:

> "Use of unique identifier
> (a) The [institution] shall make the unique identifier(s) of its registered mortgage loan originator(s) available to consumers in a manner and method practicable to the institution.
> (b) A registered mortgage loan originator shall provide his or her unique identifier to a consumer:
> (1) Upon request;
> (2) Before acting as a mortgage loan originator; and
> (3) Through the originator's initial written communication with a consumer, if any, whether on paper or electronically."

Source: NMLS Knowledge Base — [Required Use of NMLS ID](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/aboutNMLS/reference/aboutNMLS/maps/topics/nmls_uniqueID_requiredUse.html) (citing Federal Register, July 28, 2010).

> **Crucial clarifying language from the same Federal Register preamble** (quoted in the NMLS Knowledge Base):
>
> "While this provision does not require institutions to include the unique identifier on loan program descriptions, advertisements, business cards, stationary, notepads, and other similar materials, institutions are not prohibited from doing so."
>
> "The Agencies also clarify that the unique identifier may be provided orally, except pursuant to paragraph (b)(3) under which the unique identifier would be provided with the written or electronic communication."

Source: NMLS Knowledge Base — Required Use of NMLS ID (same URL above).

> **Federal baseline: the federal banking-agency rule is the floor — and it is permissive.** Under federal Regulation G / 12 CFR 1007 alone, a depository-institution MLO is not *required* to put the NMLS ID on advertising, business cards, or stationery; they only must (i) make it available upon request, (ii) provide it before acting as an MLO, and (iii) include it in initial written communications. **Most state laws are stricter** and *do* require the NMLS ID in advertising — see Part II.

### 1.4 FHFA and FHA Overlay Rules

Per the NMLS Knowledge Base:

> "The benefit of the unique identifier has been recognized in a similar capacity by the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), which is requiring Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to include the identifiers for the loan originator and loan origination company on each loan application. Additionally, the Federal Housing Administration has also begun collecting the NMLS unique identifiers of all individuals and entities participating in the origination of FHA loans."

Source: NMLS Knowledge Base — [Required Use of NMLS ID](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/aboutNMLS/reference/aboutNMLS/maps/topics/nmls_uniqueID_requiredUse.html).

> **Regulation Z (12 CFR § 1026.36(g))** also requires the loan originator organization's name and NMLS ID, plus the individual MLO's name and NMLS ID, on the credit application, the § 1026.19(e) and (f) disclosures, the note/loan contract, and the security instrument. See [12 CFR § 1026.36(g)](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/final-rules/loan-originator-compensation-final-rule-iii-2013-21/) (CFPB).

### 1.5 The CSBS/AARMR Model State Law Provision (the de facto state baseline)

The Conference of State Bank Supervisors (CSBS) and the American Association of Residential Mortgage Regulators (AARMR) Model State Law contains the "UNIQUE IDENTIFIER SHOWN" provision. The text, as adopted by the large majority of states (see e.g. Ohio Rev. Code § 1322.49, Va. 10 VAC 5-160-60, the Mass. mortgage broker rules, and the California CRMLA framework) is:

> "MSL XX.XXX.210 UNIQUE IDENTIFIER SHOWN. The unique identifier of any person originating a residential mortgage loan shall be clearly shown on all residential mortgage loan application forms, solicitations or advertisements, including business cards or websites, and any other documents as established by rule, regulation or order of the Commissioner."

Source: NMLS Knowledge Base — [Required Use of NMLS ID](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/aboutNMLS/reference/aboutNMLS/maps/topics/nmls_uniqueID_requiredUse.html).

> **Operational effect.** This is the *substantive advertising rule* that most states have enacted. The federal floor (under 12 CFR Part 1007 and the joint banking-agency rule) does not require NMLS ID on advertising; the model state provision, enacted in the majority of states, *does*. The two are reconciled: state law is the binding rule for state-licensed MLOs; federal law applies to depository-institution MLOs.

## PART II — NMLS / S.A.F.E. Act Advertising Rules

### 2.1 When Must an MLO's NMLS ID Appear in Advertising? The "Individual vs. Institutional" Distinction

The federal floor does not require the NMLS ID on *advertising*. State law generally does. The conventional state-level breakdown is:

| Level | What it covers | NMLS ID on advertising? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal banking-agency rule** (12 CFR Part 1007 and the joint §§ 34.105 / 208.105 / 365.105 / 563.105 / 610.105 / 761.105) | Depository-institution MLOs | **No — but must be available on request, before acting, and in initial written communication** | 12 CFR § 1007.103; Federal Register July 28, 2010 preamble (quoted above) |
| **CSBS/AARMR Model State Law MSL XX.XXX.210** (the *substantive advertising rule* adopted in most states) | State-licensed MLOs (individuals and companies) | **Yes — on all application forms, solicitations, advertisements, business cards, websites** | NMLS Knowledge Base — Required Use of NMLS ID |
| **Regulation Z 12 CFR § 1026.36(g)** | All loan originator organizations and individual MLOs on documents listed | **Yes — on credit application, § 1026.19(e)/(f) Loan Estimate / Closing Disclosure, note, security instrument** | 12 CFR § 1026.36(g); CFPB Loan Originator Compensation Final Rule (2013) |

#### 2.1.1 "Individual advertisement" vs. "institutional advertisement"

These terms are **not used in the federal SAFE Act or in 12 CFR Part 1007**. They come up in state-level guidance and industry compliance materials. Operationally, the distinction is:

- **Individual advertisement.** Communication that names or features a specific individual MLO (a "loan officer" by name, with NMLS ID, photo, branch, direct phone, etc.). The MLO's own NMLS Unique Identifier must appear.
- **Institutional advertisement.** Communication about the company/lender itself (e.g., the Wells Fargo brand, Rocket's "Apply Now" radio spot) that does not name an individual MLO. The institution's NMLS Unique Identifier must appear; the individual MLO NMLS ID is required at the *application* stage under Reg. Z § 1026.36(g) and at the *initial written communication* stage under the federal banking-agency rule.

> **Federal banking agencies explicitly addressed the institutional advertising question in the preamble to the joint final rule (2010):**
>
> "While this provision does not require institutions to include the unique identifier on loan program descriptions, advertisements, business cards, stationary, notepads, and other similar materials, institutions are not prohibited from doing so."
>
> Source: NMLS Knowledge Base — [Required Use of NMLS ID](https://mortgage.nationwidelicensingsystem.org/knowledge/products/nmls/pubs/aboutNMLS/reference/aboutNMLS/maps/topics/nmls_uniqueID_requiredUse.html) (Federal Register, July 28, 2010, quoted).

In practice: the *institution* (the lender or mortgage broker that holds the NMLS company ID) typically displays its NMLS ID on all advertising; the *individual MLO's* NMLS ID is required at application/closing under Reg. Z and on business cards under the model state law.

### 2.2 Lead-Generation Advertising and the "Click-to-Call" Question

A lead-generation (lead-gen) advertisement, or a "click-to-call" / "Apply Now" form on a third-party website, is treated as **advertising by the recipient MLO** if the recipient MLO has any role in:

- (i) the content of the advertisement;
- (ii) the targeting criteria or audience selection;
- (iii) the compensation paid to the lead generator; or
- (iv) the funnel that follows the consumer's submission (e.g., the MLO receives the lead, contacts the consumer, and evaluates the consumer for a loan).

Under that fact pattern, the *MLO receiving the lead* must ensure that the advertisement complies with the state advertising rules applicable to the MLO. Most state regulators (and the CFPB in the TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure context) have not formally addressed "click-to-call" lead-gen advertising in a stand-alone rule, but enforcement has consistently treated the lead-generating entity as engaged in mortgage activity if it receives compensation tied to loan outcomes.

#### 2.2.1 What makes a lead-generation site advertising "by" an MLO?

The "advertising by" attribution generally tracks these factors (drawn from the SAFE Act § 5103(b) "advertising or other means of communicating" trigger, the CSBS/AARMR Model State Law MSL XX.XXX.210, and CFPB / state enforcement actions):

- **Sponsorship / payment.** If the MLO pays for the ad or pays the lead generator, the ad is generally treated as the MLO's.
- **Endorsement.** If the MLO's name, photo, license, or NMLS ID is displayed, the ad is the MLO's.
- **Direction.** If the MLO directs the content or audience, the ad is the MLO's.
- **Receipt of compensation contingent on closing.** A site that receives a "lead fee" payable on closing (or a per-lead fee) is most likely engaged in a form of mortgage activity; the MLO-recipient is treated as the principal.

#### 2.2.2 Must a lead-generation site display an NMLS ID?

- **If the site is the MLO's site, or is held out to be, or is paid by the MLO, then yes.** The state's advertising rule (the model state provision quoted in § 1.5 above, and the state-specific implementations in Part III) applies. The NMLS ID of the licensed company and of the individual MLO must appear on the website in a "clearly shown" manner; the precise format is state-specific (e.g., Virginia requires "NMLS ID # 999999 (www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org)").
- **If the site is purely a "publisher" of editorial content with no referral relationship** (e.g., a news site that runs a banner ad for an unrelated lender), the MLO's advertising rules still apply to the *ad content* but the publisher does not need its own NMLS ID.

#### 2.2.3 NMLS Consumer Access (the public lookup)

NMLS Consumer Access ([https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org](https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org)) is the public-facing, free search portal authorized by 12 USC § 5111(d). It allows any consumer to look up:

- A state-licensed mortgage company, branch, or individual MLO
- The MLO's employment history (last 10 years)
- Publicly adjudicated disciplinary and enforcement actions
- The MLO's NMLS Unique Identifier and the company's NMLS company ID
- The state(s) in which the MLO is licensed

NMLS Consumer Access is **not** a place where a consumer applies for a loan. The consumer's application is still made on the lender's or broker's site (or on a lead-gen site that funnels the consumer to one). The Consumer Access portal is, in effect, the "license verification" tool that most state rules require to be linked from licensee advertising.

> **Subtle point — "NMLS Consumer Access" vs. "NMLS" itself.** The NMLS is the secure, behind-the-scenes state-regulator licensing system. NMLS Consumer Access is the public website. NMLS Unique Identifiers are issued in the NMLS and *displayed* on the NMLS Consumer Access website. The distinction matters for the required disclosures in § 3 below.

#### 2.2.4 Must the website itself be registered in NMLS (as a unique-identifier owner)?

A website (lead-generation site) is **not** required to obtain its own NMLS Unique Identifier *if* it does not hold itself out as a lender or mortgage broker, does not negotiate loan terms, and does not receive compensation contingent on loan closing. If any of those criteria is met, the site is engaged in mortgage activity in the relevant state(s) and must be licensed (as a mortgage broker, mortgage banker, or — in California — under the CRMLA as a "person" engaged in mortgage-lending activity, or under the CFLL as a broker) **and** must obtain an NMLS company ID (and an NMLS ID for any individual acting as an MLO on the site's behalf).

The detailed state-by-state analysis is in Part III.

### 2.3 Summary: What the Federal Floor Requires on a Lead-Generation Site Routing to a Single MLO

If the site merely:

- Asks the consumer a fixed set of factual questions (income, ZIP, debts, home value, etc.);
- Returns *preliminary, generic, non-binding* estimates computed from publicly available data;
- Does not quote or imply any specific rate, APR, payment, or term;
- Does not represent that any particular loan will be offered; and
- Routs the consumer to a *single, identified, named, NMLS-licensed MLO* whose NMLS ID and licensing statement are displayed on the site;

…then the site, in most states, can avoid itself being a "loan originator" or "mortgage broker" by *not* being compensated based on loan outcomes, *not* negotiating terms, and *not* taking applications. The site should still display (i) the MLO's NMLS ID, (ii) the lender/company's NMLS ID, (iii) the licensing statement required by the relevant state, (iv) an Equal Housing Lender logo and statement, (v) a "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer, and (vi) a clear statement of the relationship between the site and the MLO. State law may impose additional requirements — see Part III.

If the site *does* any of the following, it likely becomes a "mortgage broker" or "loan originator" in one or more states and must be licensed:

- Receives a fee contingent on loan closing;
- Quotes a specific rate, APR, payment, or term;
- Collects a "full application" (rather than preliminary qualification information);
- "Steers" the consumer to a particular loan product;
- Receives compensation in exchange for *transmitting* the consumer's information to the MLO when the transmission is more than a passive referral (e.g., the site pre-qualifies, screens, or filters the consumer before transmission).

The boundary is heavily state-specific. See Part III.

### 3.1 California (DFPI / Department of Financial Protection and Innovation)

#### 3.1.1 Primary citations

- **Statute.** California Residential Mortgage Lending Act (CRMLA), Cal. Fin. Code Division 20, §§ 50000 *et seq.* (commencing at § 50000). Advertising-specific statutory provisions: Cal. Fin. Code § 50002 (advertising disclosures) and § 50205 (NMLS Consumer Access / unique-identifier hyperlink); § 50204 prohibits material misstatements.

(The "10 CCR § 2840 series" cited in the user's prompt is the *pre-2011* numbering and has been superseded. The current advertising rules are §§ 1950.204.3 and 1950.204.4.) Source: Cornell LII cross-reference for 10 CCR Chapter 3, Subchapter 11.5 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california).
- **Regulator.** Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI), 2101 Arena Blvd., Sacramento, CA 95834. Predecessor: Department of Business Oversight; prior: Department of Corporations.

#### 3.1.2 Direct quote — 10 CCR § 1950.204.3 (Prohibited Advertising)

> "(a) A residential mortgage lender, mortgage servicer, residential mortgage lender and servicer, or mortgage loan originator shall not advertise that it or any of its officers, employees, or agents are 'bonded,' 'supervised by,' 'regulated by,' 'audited by' or 'examined by' the State of California or any agency thereof.
>
> (b) A residential mortgage lender, mortgage servicer, residential mortgage lender and servicer, or mortgage loan originator shall refer to its licensure under the Act in any written or printed communication or any communication by means of recorded telephone messages, telephonic or electronic media, or spoken on radio, television or similar communications media, only by the following statement: 'Licensed by the Department of Business Oversight under the California Residential Mortgage Lending Act.'"

Source: 10 CCR § 1950.204.3 (current text, Register 2026 No. 33) via [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-1950.204.3) and Westlaw [calregs](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/).

> **Note on the agency name.** The rule, as published, still reads "Department of Business Oversight." The DBO was renamed the DFPI in 2020 (Cal. Fin. Code § 300). The legislative renaming operates automatically; the conservative reading is to use the rule's literal text, but licensees should follow current DFPI guidance (which uses the DFPI name).

#### 3.1.3 Direct quote — 10 CCR § 1950.204.4 ("Blind" Advertising Prohibited)

> "A residential mortgage lender, residential mortgage lender and servicer, mortgage servicer, or mortgage loan originator shall not use 'blind' advertisements. 'Blind' advertising is an advertisement used to solicit business that gives only a telephone number, post office or newspaper box number, or name other than that of the licensee."

Source: 10 CCR § 1950.204.4 via [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-1950.204.4).

Source: [California Financial Code § 50002](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/) (current).

#### 3.1.5 NMLS Unique Identifier / NMLS Consumer Access (Cal. Fin. Code § 50205(b))

Cal. Fin. Code § 50205(b) (added by SB 1248 / Stats. 2016, ch. 473) requires every residential mortgage lender and loan originator licensed under the CRMLA who maintains a "commercial loan solicitation, lender, or originator communication" (e.g., website, social media, advertisement) to display:

- (i) the licensee's NMLS Unique Identifier,
- (ii) the NMLS Unique Identifier of any individual MLO acting on the licensee's behalf, and
- (iii) a hyperlink or other method allowing the consumer to access the NMLS Consumer Access website (https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org).

This is the *operative* California NMLS-in-advertising rule; it supplements (does not replace) the federal SAFE Act and the CSBS/AARMR Model State Law provision.

#### 3.1.6 Prohibited claims in advertising (California)

- "Bonded by the State of California" / "Supervised by" / "Regulated by" / "Audited by" / "Examined by" the State of California (10 CCR § 1950.204.3(a)).
- "Blind" advertising (only a phone number, P.O. box, or name other than that of the licensee) (10 CCR § 1950.204.4).
- Material misstatements or material omissions (Cal. Fin. Code § 50204).
- Unfair, deceptive, or abusive acts or practices under the California Consumer Financial Protection Law (CCFPL), Cal. Fin. Code § 90000 *et seq.* The DFPI's December 13, 2023 *Advertising and Marketing Guidance* addresses CCFPL UDAAP-style analysis for mortgage advertising. (Direct fetch of the December 13, 2023 guidance document was blocked by the DFPI's web-application firewall during this research; the document exists and is referenced in DFPI bulletins.)
- Quoting a specific rate or APR without Reg. Z disclosures (12 CFR § 1026.24 and § 1026.36).
- "Guaranteed approval" / "lowest rate" claims that are deceptive.

The DFPI published *Advertising and Marketing Guidance* on December 13, 2023, addressing mortgage advertising and UDAAP under the CCFPL. The guidance is hosted on the DFPI website. #### 3.1.8 Lead-generation / mortgage-broker licensing analysis (California)

- Cal. Fin. Code § 50003(m) defines "mortgage loan originator" broadly, including any natural person who (a) takes a residential mortgage loan application or (b) "offers or negotiates terms of a residential mortgage loan" for compensation or gain. The DFPI has historically treated a person who "offers" terms — even through a lead-generation site that produces preliminary affordability estimates — as acting as an MLO unless an explicit exemption applies.
- A person who connects consumers with lenders *for compensation* is a mortgage broker under California law and must be licensed under either the California Financing Law (CFLL) or the CRMLA, depending on the activity.
- A *purely informational* site that (i) only performs calculations using publicly available data, (ii) does not represent that any particular loan will be offered, and (iii) does not "negotiate, arrange, or offer" loan terms, may avoid licensure. The moment a site collects identifying information for transmission to a specific MLO in exchange for compensation, the analysis shifts toward a "lead generation" service that the DFPI has historically treated as implicating broker activity. RESPA § 8 (12 USC § 2607) federal kickback prohibitions also apply.
- The website **must display** (per Cal. Fin. Code § 50205 and 10 CCR § 1950.204.3(b)) the licensing statement for the lender/MLO that will receive the lead and the MLO's NMLS Unique Identifier with a working link to https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org. The website should also carry Equal Housing Lender language and a "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer (the latter is required by the SAFE Act / Reg. Z for any rate quotation that could be interpreted as an offer; it is also good practice under § 50204 of the Cal. Fin. Code to avoid "material misstatement" claims).

#### 3.3.7 Lead-generation analysis (Florida)

- Transmits that information to a single MLO or mortgage broker in Florida; and
- Receives a per-lead fee or any fee contingent on loan outcome

…will be treated as acting as a mortgage broker under Fla. Stat. § 494.0025(2) and must be licensed. Florida's OFR has been particularly aggressive in pursuing unlicensed lead-generation activity in 2022-2025 (see Part IV).

## PART V — Summary of Practical Requirements for the Lead-Generation Website

### 5.1 What the Website Must Display

Based on the federal baseline and the state-specific rules catalogued above, a U.S. consumer mortgage qualification diagnostic website that captures leads and routes them to a single licensed MLO should display, at minimum:

1. **The MLO's name and NMLS Unique Identifier** (the individual's ID, not just the company's).
2. **The lender/company's name and NMLS Unique Identifier** (the company ID, not just the individual's).
3. **A working hyperlink to https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org** (the NMLS Consumer Access website). In Virginia, the exact format is "NMLS ID # 999999 (www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org)".
4. **The licensing statement** required by the state (e.g., "Licensed by the Department of Financial Protection and Innovation under the California Residential Mortgage Lending Act" in California; analogous statements in other states).
5. **Equal Housing Lender logo and statement** ("Equal Housing Lender" or the three-house logo with the phrase "Equal Housing Opportunity").
6. **A "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer** on every page where the site displays any rate, APR, payment, or term.
7. **A clear statement of the relationship between the site and the MLO** (e.g., "This site is operated by [Site Operator] and routes your information to [MLO Name], NMLS ID # 999999, a licensed mortgage loan originator.").
8. **A privacy policy** that discloses how the consumer's information will be used, shared, and stored.
9. **A "click-to-call" / lead-routing disclosure** that explains that the consumer is being connected to a single MLO, not a marketplace, and that the MLO will contact the consumer.

### 5.2 What the Website Should NOT Do

To avoid itself becoming a "mortgage broker" or "loan originator" and triggering licensing requirements:

- **Do not quote a specific rate, APR, payment, or term.** Preliminary, generic, non-binding estimates (e.g., "You may qualify for a loan in the range of $X to $Y") are generally acceptable; specific rates are not.
- **Do not represent that any particular loan will be offered.** The site should make clear that the consumer is being pre-qualified, not approved.
- **Do not "steer" the consumer to a particular loan product.** The site should not recommend a specific loan type (e.g., "We recommend a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage") unless the recommendation is based on the consumer's stated preferences and is clearly non-binding.
- **Do not receive a fee contingent on loan closing.** The site should receive a flat fee for the lead (or no fee at all) rather than a percentage of the loan amount or a fee payable on closing.
- **Do not collect a "full application."** The site should collect only preliminary qualification information (income, ZIP, debts, home value, etc.) and route the consumer to the MLO for the full application.
- **Do not represent that the site is "supervised by," "regulated by," "audited by," or "examined by" any state agency.**
- **Do not use "blind" advertising.** The site must identify the licensed MLO and lender, not just a phone number or P.O. box.
- **Do not misrepresent affiliation with a bank, credit union, or government agency.**
- **Do not advertise "guaranteed approval" or "lowest rates."**

The website must hold its own NMLS company ID, display that ID on every page that accepts consumer information, and comply with the state's advertising rules (including NMLS ID disclosure, "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer, and Equal Housing Lender statement).

If the website (i) only performs preliminary qualification calculations using publicly available data, (ii) does not quote a specific rate, APR, payment, or term, (iii) does not represent that any particular loan will be offered, and (iv) receives only a flat fee (or no fee) for the lead, the website may avoid itself becoming a "mortgage broker" in most states. However, the website should still display the MLO's and lender's NMLS IDs, a working hyperlink to NMLS Consumer Access, the state's licensing statement, an Equal Housing Lender logo, and a "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer.
