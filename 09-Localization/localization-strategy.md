# PART 9 — LOCALIZATION STRATEGY

## 9.1 The Five Options

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | One national website | Simplest; broadest SEO; one set of compliance | Doesn't address state-specific programs; license complexity; less "local" trust |
| **B** | One state only | Deepest local focus; easier license; clearer messaging | Smaller TAM; harder to scale; miss out on cross-state search traffic |
| **C** | National site with state pages | Broad reach + state specificity; programmatic SEO | Risk of thin doorway pages; need real state content; harder compliance |
| **D** | National brand + local funnels | Best of both; city/state landing pages; deep local | Most complex; requires per-state setup; heavy content production |
| **E** | Separate sites per market | Maximum local focus; cleanest license | Massive overhead; slow growth; unaffordable for one MLO |

## 9.2 Recommended Approach for MVP: **Option C (State-Specific Landing Pages)**

But with strict guardrails to avoid thin doorway pages.

### Why Option C
- Captures both broad "why can't I qualify" SEO traffic AND state-specific searches
- One site, one CMS, one funnel — manageable
- State pages become useful, not just SEO bait
- Scales to city/zip pages later

### Why NOT Option A (purely national)
- "First time home buyer programs [state]" has 30-50% of relevant traffic
- 70% of mortgage searches have local intent
- Mortgage is state-licensed — single-license national site looks suspect
- Local trust matters in mortgage

### Why NOT Option B (single state)
- Limits growth ceiling
- State-specific searches are only part of the addressable market
- "Why can't I qualify" is not state-specific
- One-state limits the long-term business

### Why NOT Option D/E (heavy localization)
- Way too much overhead for one MLO
- Requires per-state legal review
- Hard to manage compliance
- Won't be ROI-positive for years

## 9.3 State Page Strategy — Avoid Doorway Pages

### ❌ NEVER do this (doorway page):
```
/california-mortgage-readiness
   "California Mortgage Readiness Check" (just state name swapped)
/texas-mortgage-readiness
   "Texas Mortgage Readiness Check" (just state name swapped)
/florida-mortgage-readiness
   "Florida Mortgage Readiness Check" (just state name swapped)
```
Google penalizes this. Users hate it. It's a Google SpamBrain violation.

### ✅ DO this (real state content):

Each state page includes:
1. **State-specific loan programs** (e.g., CalHFA for CA, My First Texas Home for TX, FL HFA for FL, etc.)
2. **State-specific down payment assistance**
3. **State-specific tax considerations** (property tax rates, transfer taxes, mortgage interest deduction state nuances)
4. **State-specific first-time buyer programs**
5. **State-specific median prices, payment examples**
6. **State-specific insurance considerations** (hurricane, earthquake, wildfire, etc.)
7. **State licensing and regulatory notes** (e.g., MD has unique licensing, NY has high-cost area limits)
8. **Local MLO info** (NMLS, state license #)

Each state page is **200-500 words minimum of unique content** with real local information. This is real value, not doorway spam.

## 9.4 MVP State Selection (Recommended)

### Phase 1: Launch in 1 home state + national SEO

Pick the state where the MLO is licensed and has strongest network.

Criteria for state selection:
- Where are you already licensed?
- Where do you have a sphere of influence?
- Where are property prices reasonable (more buyers = more searches)?
- Where is mortgage regulation straightforward?

**Common recommendations:**
- **Texas** — no state income tax, large population, growing market
- **Florida** — no state income tax, lots of first-time buyers, retirees
- **California** — large market, lots of first-time buyers, but high prices
- **Arizona, Nevada, North Carolina, Georgia** — popular relocation destinations
- **Midwest states (OH, MI, IN)** — affordable, but smaller margins

### Phase 2: Add 2-3 more states with strong SEO
- States with high cost-of-living crisis (CA, NY, MA, WA, OR, CO, HI)
- States with high denial rates (per Fed data)
- States with major population centers

### Phase 3: National SEO with state pages for all 50 states (V3)
- Programmatically generate state pages with real, unique content
- Each state gets 200-500 words of genuine content
- Lower-effort than per-state but still substantive

## 9.5 Compliance Implications of State Pages

### License disclosures
- Each state page must show:
  - State-specific license number
  - State regulatory body (e.g., CA DBO, NY DFS, FL OFR)
  - Equal Housing Lender logo
  - NMLS unique identifier
- If the MLO is NOT licensed in a state, that state page should NOT exist (or should redirect to "we don't currently serve [state], but here's how to find a local pro")

### State advertising rules
Some states have unique advertising rules:
- **California (DBO):** Specific disclosure language required for mortgage ads
- **New York (DFS):** Strict advertising rules, including pre-approval disclosures
- **Massachusetts:** Special reverse mortgage rules
- **Maryland:** Unique mortgage lender licensing
- **Texas (SML):** Specific advertising rules
- **Florida (OFR):** Specific advertising rules
- **Illinois (IDFPR):** Specific rules
- **Pennsylvania:** Specific rules

**Action:** Have a mortgage compliance attorney review state-specific landing pages for each state where you operate. Budget: $1,500-3,000 per state for legal review.

## 9.6 Local SEO Strategy

### Google Business Profile
- Create a Google Business Profile for the business
- Use a real address (not a virtual office) if possible — Google is cracking down on virtual offices for financial services
- If using a home office, ensure compliance with local zoning
- Get listed in:
  - Google Business Profile
  - Bing Places
  - Apple Maps
  - Yelp
  - Better Business Bureau
  - Zillow (where still available)
  - Realtor.com (where still available)
  - Local Chamber of Commerce

### Reviews
- Actively ask happy past clients for reviews
- Respond to all reviews
- Reviews are a top local SEO ranking factor
- Target: 25+ 5-star Google reviews in year 1

### Local citations
- Ensure NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistency across all directories
- Use a service like Moz Local, Yext, BrightLocal

## 9.7 Multi-State Compliance

### If you serve multiple states
- License in each state before marketing
- Maintain separate disclosures per state
- Comply with state-specific rate sheets
- Watch for state-specific programs (CalHFA, FL HFA, etc.)
- Track which state the lead is in for proper routing

### Best practice
- Store lead's state
- Route lead to appropriate LO licensed in that state
- If you have multiple LOs, dispatch to the right one
- If solo, only accept leads from states where you're licensed

## 9.9 Localized Conversion Optimization

### On state pages, add:
- "Serving [State] since [year]"
- "[State] homeowner photos (with permission)"
- Local market data
- Local loan program spotlights
- State-specific disclaimers

### City pages (V3+)
- "/mortgage-readiness/[city-state]"
- Only for cities with enough population (50k+)
- Include median prices, school district notes, neighborhood quirks
- 300-500 words minimum per city
- Avoid creating thousands of thin city pages

## 9.10 Summary Recommendation

**MVP (V1):** Single state, deep focus, license-clean, compliance-clean
**V2:** 2-5 states with state pages
**V3:** National SEO with 50 state pages (programmatic but substantive)

Always ensure:
- Each state page has 200+ words of real local content
- MLO is licensed in each state marketed
- State-specific disclosures present
- No doorway pages
- No fake local addresses
- Compliance attorney review before going live in each state
