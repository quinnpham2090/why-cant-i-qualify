Mortgage decisions feel opaque because they are usually delivered as a yes or
no without the middle part. The middle part is seven factors, evaluated
together. Here they are, in the order most files are built.

## 1. Income stability

Not just how much — how *reliable*. Two years of steady W-2 work is the
simple case. Bonuses, overtime, commission, and self-employment income
typically need a two-year history to count fully. A new job in the same field
with a signed offer letter? Usually fine. A brand-new career? Harder.

## 2. Debt load

Your monthly obligations against your income — the DTI story from our earlier
post. Car payments and student loans do most of the damage here, not credit
cards with small minimums.

## 3. Credit profile

Scores matter, but so do events. A bankruptcy, foreclosure, or short sale
starts a clock — often two to four years depending on the program. What
matters most is where you are on that timeline, and what you have done since.

## 4. Cash and savings

Down payment plus closing costs plus reserves (months of payments left over
after closing). Gift funds are usually allowed with a letter explaining where
the money came from. Large unexplained deposits get questioned — paper trails
matter.

## 5. Payment fit

The estimated housing payment against your income. This is where a realistic
price range comes from — not a maximum you should spend, but a number that
fits the rest of your life.

## 6. The property itself

Condos in buildings with litigation or heavy investor concentration, manufactured
homes built before 1976, homes on leased land, or properties in flood zones
with special requirements — the property can be the reason a file struggles,
no matter how strong your finances are.

## 7. Documentation

The quiet killer. Every fact above has to be *provable*. Pay stubs, W-2s,
bank statements, letters of explanation. Files rarely fail on facts; they fail
on paperwork.

## Why this matters more than the yes/no

When a file comes back with a negative answer, it is almost never about all
seven areas — it is usually one or two. The useful question is never "can I
buy?" but "which of the seven is furthest behind, and what is the shortest
path to move it?"
