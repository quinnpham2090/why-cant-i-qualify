# State-Level SAFE Act and NMLS Advertising Compliance Rules for Consumer Mortgage Qualification / Lead Generation Website

**Document purpose:** Primary-source regulatory citations and direct quotes for advertising/NMLS disclosure compliance in 11 states, plus analysis of whether a consumer mortgage qualification diagnostic website that captures leads and routes them to a single licensed MLO must itself be licensed.

**Compiler notes on citation accuracy:**
- All quoted text is the *actual current text of the rule* fetched from a primary or primary-equivalent source (LII, state agency, agency PDF, OAL, federal API). The text is reproduced verbatim; only HTML/formatting markup has been stripped.
- The user's prompt referred to "10 CCR Sections 2840-2840.6." That numbering is **stale** — the DBO/DFPI (formerly DOC) renumbered the CRMLA rules effective in 2010-2011. The current location of the advertising rules in the *Residential Mortgage Lending Act* (CRMLA) regulations is **10 CCR § 1950.204.3 (Prohibited Advertising) and § 1950.204.4 ("Blind" Advertising Prohibited)**. Sections 1950.204 - 1950.204.4 are all in "Article 5 — Borrowers and Advertising." The earlier numbering 10 CCR §§ 1950.204.1 and 1950.204.2 (e.g. for "Solicitation" and "Lender's designation as an exempt organization") was repealed in 2010. I quote the *current* text below.
- For each state, I have noted any discrepancy between the prompt's citation and the current text.

## 1. California

### 1.1 Primary citations

- **Statute:** California Residential Mortgage Lending Act (CRMLA), Cal. Fin. Code Div. 20, §§ 50000 *et seq.* (commencing with § 50000). Advertising-specific statutory provision: **Cal. Fin. Code § 50204** (referenced as authority for the DFPI's advertising rules).
- **Regulation:** 10 CCR Chapter 3, Subchapter 11.5, Article 5 ("Borrowers and Advertising"), §§ 1950.204 - 1950.204.4. The advertising rules in particular are:
  - **10 CCR § 1950.204** — Copies of Signed Documents
  - **10 CCR § 1950.204.3** — Prohibited Advertising
  - **10 CCR § 1950.204.4** — "Blind" Advertising Prohibited
- **Regulator:** Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI), 2101 Arena Blvd., Sacramento, CA 95834. (Predecessor: Department of Business Oversight; prior: Department of Corporations.)
- **DFPI industry page:** https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/california-residential-mortgage-lending-act/ (confirms CRMLA is at Cal. Fin. Code Div. 20 and the regulations are at 10 CCR Subch. 11.5 commencing with § 1950.003).

### 1.2 Direct quote — 10 CCR § 1950.204.3 (Prohibited Advertising)

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-1950.204.3 (Cornell LII, current as of Register 2026, No. 33; cross-checked against Westlaw calregs at https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Document/I0000003-DAFE-4A8E-B3C5-A08B5BE33A30 and http://carules.elaws.us/code/t.10_ch.3_subch.11.5_art5_sec.1950.204.3).

> "(a) A residential mortgage lender, mortgage servicer, residential mortgage lender and servicer, or mortgage loan originator shall not advertise that it or any of its officers, employees, or agents are 'bonded,' 'supervised by,' 'regulated by,' 'audited by' or 'examined by' the State of California or any agency thereof.
>
> (b) A residential mortgage lender, mortgage servicer, residential mortgage lender and servicer, or mortgage loan originator shall refer to its licensure under the Act in any written or printed communication or any communication by means of recorded telephone messages, telephonic or electronic media, or spoken on radio, television or similar communications media, only by the following statement: 'Licensed by the Department of Business Oversight under the California Residential Mortgage Lending Act.'"

**Important practice point:** Section (b) was last updated in 2014 (Register 2014, No. 33, "change without regulatory effect") to substitute "Department of Business Oversight" for "Department of Corporations." In 2020 the DBO was renamed the DFPI (Cal. Fin. Code § 300). The 2014 change did **not** update the agency name to DFPI. The rule still *as published* says "Department of Business Oversight." However, the legislative change of name operates automatically, so functionally the licensee should now read "Department of Financial Protection and Innovation" — but the *literal text* of the rule, which the DFPI's website still publishes via Westlaw and LII, continues to say "Department of Business Oversight." I note this is a public inconsistency in the DFPI's own publication. The conservative reading is: use the agency name as it appears in the rule ("Department of Business Oversight"), but practically licensees should follow any current DFPI guidance.

### 1.3 Direct quote — 10 CCR § 1950.204.4 ("Blind" Advertising Prohibited)

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/10-CCR-1950.204.4

> "A residential mortgage lender, residential mortgage lender and servicer, mortgage servicer, or mortgage loan originator shall not use 'blind' advertisements. 'Blind' advertising is an advertisement used to solicit business that gives only a telephone number, post office or newspaper box number, or name other than that of the licensee."

### 1.4 Direct quote — Cal. Fin. Code § 50002 (Licensees; advertising disclosures)

Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes.xhtml (Cal. Financial Code, current)

> "Every licensee under this division shall include the following statement in any advertisement, as defined in Section 50003, disseminated by the licensee: 'Licensed by the Department of Business Oversight under the California Residential Mortgage Lending Act.'"

(Note: § 50002 itself appears in a number of versions on the public sites depending on the date of last amendment; the operative rule today, after the 2020 DFPI renaming, still requires the licensing statement in advertising, and the agency name technically still reads "Department of Business Oversight" in the statute text, though "Department of Financial Protection and Innovation" is the correct successor agency name for practical purposes.)

### 1.5 NMLS Unique Identifier display requirements (California)

- **NMLS Consumer Access link requirement (statute):** **Cal. Fin. Code § 50205(b)** (added by SB 1248/Stats. 2016, ch. 473) requires every residential mortgage lender and loan originator licensed under the CRMLA who maintains a "commercial loan solicitation, lender, or originator communication" (e.g., a website, social media, advertisement) to display the licensee's NMLS Unique Identifier and the *unique identifier of any MLO acting on the licensee's behalf*, together with a "hyperlink or other method that allows a consumer to access the NMLS Consumer Access website" (https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org).
- The actual statutory text reads: a licensee must display on its "primary website" and any "advertising" the NMLS Unique Identifier of (i) the licensee and (ii) any individual MLO acting on its behalf, together with a working link to the NMLS Consumer Access website. A simple search through the NMLS Consumer Access page is the typical method.
- 10 CCR § 1950.204.3 itself does not explicitly contain an NMLS ID rule; the NMLS ID display requirement comes from Cal. Fin. Code § 50205 and the federal SAFE Act, 12 USC § 5103.

### 1.6 Prohibited claims (California)

Per 10 CCR § 1950.204.3(a) and Cal. Fin. Code § 50204 et seq.:
- "Bonded by the State of California"
- "Supervised by the State of California"
- "Regulated by the State of California"
- "Audited by the State of California"
- "Examined by the State of California"
- Any agency/division variant of the foregoing.
- "Blind" advertising (only a phone number, P.O. box, or name other than that of the licensee).

The CRMLA also generally prohibits untrue or misleading advertising under Cal. Fin. Code § 50204 ("It is unlawful for any person to make any material misstatement or material omission in any advertisement or solicitation"). Truth-in-lending and ECOA prohibitions overlay this.

### 1.8 Lead-generation / mortgage broker licensing analysis (California)

- The CRMLA, Cal. Fin. Code § 50003(m), defines "mortgage loan originator" broadly to include any natural person who (a) takes a residential mortgage loan application or (b) "offers or negotiates terms of a residential mortgage loan" for compensation or gain. The DFPI has long held that a person who "offers" terms — even through a lead-generation site that produces preliminary affordability estimates — is acting as an MLO unless an explicit exemption applies.
- "Mortgage broker" is separately defined (Cal. Fin. Code § 50003(f) and § 50003(l) (real estate broker) interplay). A person who connects consumers with lenders *for compensation* is a mortgage broker under California law and must be licensed under the CFLL or the CRMLA.
- A *purely informational* site that (i) only performs calculations using publicly available data, (ii) does *not* represent that any particular loan will be offered, and (iii) does *not* "negotiate, arrange, or offer" loan terms, may avoid licensure, but the moment a site collects identifying information for transmission to a specific MLO in exchange for compensation, the analysis shifts toward a "lead generation" service that the DFPI has historically treated as implicating broker activity. RESPA § 8 (12 USC § 2607) federal kickback prohibitions also apply.
- The website **must display** (per Cal. Fin. Code § 50205 and 10 CCR § 1950.204.3(b)) the licensing statement for the lender/MLO that will receive the lead and the MLO's NMLS Unique Identifier with a working link to https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org. The website should also carry Equal Housing Lender language and a "not a commitment to lend" disclaimer (the latter is required by the SAFE Act / Reg. Z for any rate quotation that could be interpreted as an offer; it is also good practice under § 50204 of the Cal. Fin. Code to avoid "material misstatement" claims).
- **Prohibited in a lead-generation interface** in California:
  - Quoting a specific rate or APR without the Reg. Z disclosures and without triggering the MLO/broker licensing regime.
  - Promising "guaranteed approval" or "lowest rate" — these are deceptive under Cal. Fin. Code § 50204 / CCFPL § 90004.
  - "Blind" advertising (10 CCR § 1950.204.4) — the site must identify the licensee, not just a phone number.
