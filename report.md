## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The U.S. mortgage market in 2024–2026 is defined by a persistent **affordability crisis layered on top of tightened qualification standards**. The 30-year fixed mortgage rate sits at **6.66–6.75%** as of August 2026 (Freddie Mac PMMS / Mortgage News Daily), nearly double the 2.96% pandemic-era trough. The median existing-home sale price of **$431,400** (NAR, July 2026) requires an income of roughly $122,000 to afford at the median, while the U.S. median household income sits at **$83,730** (Census, 2024 ACS).

**For a consumer-facing qualification diagnostic, the four pillars of mortgage failure are:**

1. **Affordability problems** (the dominant story) — payment-to-income ratios at 30-year highs; saving for a down payment requires 9+ years for typical first-time buyers
2. **Qualification problems** — DTI caps, FICO cutoffs, and rising lender overlays deny otherwise creditworthy applicants
3. **Documentation problems** — self-employment, complex income, condo/HOA, and property insurance paperwork
4. **Confidence/decision problems** — consumer confusion about preapproval vs. prequalification, shopping behavior, lead-generation mistrust

Below is the sourced data backing each pillar.

## 5. COMMON REASONS FOR MORTGAGE DENIAL

### 5.1 Regulation C / HMDA Denial Reason Codes (12 CFR Part 1003.4(a)(16))

The HMDA-required reason-for-denial field allows up to 4 reasons per application, drawn from Regulation B (ECOA) Appendix C model form categories:

- Debt-to-income ratio
- Employment history
- Credit history
- Insufficient collateral (low appraisal, high LTV)
- Unverifiable income
- Incomplete credit application
- Insufficient cash to close
- Unacceptable property
- Other (with explanation)

Source: <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1003>

### 5.2 Fed "Reasons for Denial" Survey Data (most recent comprehensive)

The Federal Reserve Board conducts a **Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF)** every 3 years; the most recent published module on credit rejection was the 2023 SCF. | Reason | Approximate share of denials |
|---|---|
| Debt-to-income ratio (DTI) too high | **~30-35%** |

| Incomplete credit history (thin file, no score) | **~10%** |
| Employment / income issues (job tenure, gaps, self-employed) | **~10-15%** |

## 6. DTI PROBLEMS — TYPICAL LIMITS BY LOAN TYPE

### 6.1 Conventional (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac)

- **Standard back-end DTI cap: 36%** (with strong compensating factors up to 45%, and up to 50% for DU Refi Plus)
- Front-end (housing) DTI typically 28-31%
- **Source:** Fannie Mae Selling Guide (<https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/>) DU underwriting engine output. DTI is calculated as monthly debt obligations (including the new mortgage P&I, T&I, HOA, and minimum required payments on revolving/installment debt) divided by gross monthly income.

### 6.2 FHA (Federal Housing Administration)

- **Standard back-end DTI cap: 43%**; with compensating factors (cash reserves, low LTV, strong residual income, energy efficiency), up to **56.9%** is allowed (per FHA Mortgagee Letters and TOTAL Scorecard).
- Front-end ratio 31% guideline.
- **Source:** HUD FHA Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1, <https://www.hud.gov/>

### 6.3 VA (Veterans Affairs)

- **No fixed DTI cap**, but residual income must meet VA standards (varying by family size and region).
- Guideline: back-end DTI ≤ 41% preferred; above 41% requires strong residual income and compensating factors.
- **Source:** VA Lender's Handbook, VA Pamphlet 26-7.

### 6.4 USDA (Rural Development)

- **29/41 ratios** (front-end 29%, back-end 41%) with flexibility for compensating factors.
- **Source:** USDA HB-1-3555, <https://www.rd.usda.gov/>

### 6.5 Jumbo

- Typically **36/43 DTI**, but many lenders allow 40-45% with reserves (6-12 months PITI) and high credit scores (720+).
- **Source:** Individual lender overlays; e.g., Rocket Pro, UWM, NewFi, Angel Oak.

### 6.6 Non-QM / Bank Statement / DSCR

- DTI often 50%+, calculated via bank deposits (12 or 24 months) or DSCR ratio (typically 1.0-1.25 minimum rental income / PITIA).
- **Source:** NewFi Wholesale, Deephaven Mortgage, Angel Oak product guides.

### 6.7 What DTI Includes (and the Hidden Debts That Trip People Up)

DTI is **gross monthly** debt divided by **gross monthly income**. It captures:
- New mortgage P&I
- Property taxes + homeowners insurance + HOA
- Minimum payments on revolving debt (even if borrower pays in full)
- Auto loans/leases
- Student loan minimums (1% of balance for FHA; greater of 1% or amortizing payment for conventional)
- Child support, alimony (if court-ordered)
- Other installment debt

**Common DTI-killing surprises:** new car loan (adds $400-700/mo), 401k loan repayments, BNPL (Buy Now Pay Later) minimums, child support modification.

- **VantageScore 4.0 now fully implemented (Apr 22, 2026):** *"Following FHFA's July 2025 approval of VantageScore 4.0 for use in Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac mortgages, today's announcements confirm that implementation is in place at Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and additionally that the FHA has accepted VantageScore 4.0 and commenced an implementation."* <https://www.vantagescore.com/resources/knowledge-center/fhfa-director-and-hud-secretary-jointly-announce-vantagescore-4-0-implementation-for-fannie-mae-freddie-mac-and-fha>

### 7.3 Common Credit Issues That Trigger Denial

- **Score below minimum** for the program
- **Recent late payments** (30+ days in last 12 months, especially multiple)
- **Collections / charge-offs** (medical, credit card)
- **High credit utilization** (30%+ of credit limits used)
- **Recent new credit** (multiple auto/credit card inquiries, new accounts in last 6 months)
- **Judgments, tax liens, bankruptcies** (waiting periods: Ch. 7 BK = 4 yrs conventional / 2 yrs FHA; foreclosure = 7 yrs / 3 yrs)
- **Insufficient trade lines / thin file**

## 8. SELF-EMPLOYED INCOME PROBLEMS

### 8.1 Standard Documentation Requirements (Conventional)

- **2 years of personal tax returns** (Form 1040)
- **2 years of business tax returns** (Schedule C, K-1, or 1120/1120-S)
- **Year-to-date P&L** signed by CPA or borrower
- **Year-to-date balance sheet**
- **2 months of bank statements** (all pages, all accounts)
- **Business license / CPA letter** confirming ongoing business

### 8.2 Typical Problems

- **Declining income** (most recent year's net income must be used; 2-year averaging not allowed if declining)
- **Large non-cash deductions** (depreciation, depletion) that inflate write-offs
- **Business losses** in any of the 2 most recent years
- **Excessive business write-offs** flagging income inconsistency
- **Recent business formation** (less than 2 years history requires additional documentation)
- **High Schedule C expenses** vs. industry norm
- **Mix of W-2 and self-employed income** that doesn't add up cleanly

### 8.3 Alternative Documentation (Non-QM / Bank Statement)

- **Bank Statement Loans:** 12 or 24 months of personal or business bank statements; underwriter averages deposits, may add back identified non-income transfers
- **1099 Only:** based on the highest year of 1099 income
- **Asset Depletion:** Net worth divided by loan term (typically 360 months) = qualifying income
- **DSCR:** Property rental income / PITIA = DSCR ratio (1.0-1.25 minimum)

**Source:** NewFi Wholesale, Deephaven Mortgage, Angel Oak product matrices.

## 9. ASSET / DOWN-PAYMENT PROBLEMS

### 9.1 Minimum Down Payment by Loan Type

| Loan Type | Min Down | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional (Fannie/Freddie) | **3%** | HomeReady / Home Possible; 5% standard; 10%+ for 2nd homes |
| FHA | **3.5%** | 580+ FICO; 10% if 500-579 |
| VA | **0%** | Veterans with entitlement |
| USDA | **0%** | Rural / suburban areas only |
| Jumbo | **10-20%** | Lender-dependent |
| Non-QM | **10-20%** | Higher reserves required |

### 9.2 Gift Funds

- **Conventional:** 100% of down payment can be a gift from relative, fiancé, domestic partner; gift letter required, donor's bank statement showing withdrawal and recipient's deposit
- **FHA:** 100% of down payment + closing costs from family member or approved source
- **VA:** 100% gift funds allowed from family
- **USDA:** 100% gift funds allowed

### 9.3 Down Payment Assistance (DPA) Programs

- **State Housing Finance Agencies** (HFAs) in 50 states offer programs: forgivable second mortgages, low-rate second mortgages, matched savings
- **HUD HOME program**
- **FHA Section 203(h)** (Presidential disaster areas, 100% financing)
- **FHA Section 203(k)** (Rehab rolled into mortgage)
- **State/local programs:** CalHFA, FL Hometown Heroes (FL HFA), OH Hero, NJ HMFA, etc.
- **Employer-Assisted Housing** (Boeing, Amazon, university programs in some metros)

### 9.4 Reserve Requirements

- **Conventional:** 2-6 months PITI depending on LTV and FICO
- **FHA:** 1-3 months from verified assets
- **VA:** Typically 2 months reserves
- **Jumbo:** 6-12 months reserves common
- **Non-QM:** 3-6+ months

### 9.5 Documentation Gotchas

- **Large deposits** unexplained (>$1,000 or >50% of monthly income) must be sourced
- **Earnest money** from non-payroll source must be explained
- **Crypto, stock, retirement account withdrawals** are usually counted at 60-70% liquidation value
- **Funds must be seasoned** 60-90 days (recent large deposit without explanation is denied)
- **Indebtedness for pledged assets** (borrowed against 401k, margin loan against stock) reduces usable amount

## 10. DOCUMENTATION PROBLEMS

### 10.1 Top Documentation Issues (ICE Mortgage Monitor & Lender Reports)

- **Pay stubs missing or insufficient** (need most recent 30 days)
- **Tax transcripts** that don't match what's on the application
- **1099 vs. W-2 inconsistencies** (gig economy workers)
- **VOE (Verification of Employment) failures** — most common in past 2 years: <https://www.theworknumber.com> / Equifax Workforce Solutions
- **Self-employed income decline** (most recent year used if declining)
- **Bank statement illegible / incomplete** (must be all pages, including blank pages)
- **Gift fund sourcing** (donor must be related, evidence of withdrawal and deposit)
- **Divorce decree / child support** (court orders required for alimony and child support disclosure)
- **Outstanding judgments / liens** (must be paid or subordinated)
- **Fraud alerts / credit freezes** (must be resolved before underwriting proceeds)
- **Missing signatures / initials** (consumers are often surprised by the re-signing burden)

### 10.2 Common Post-Approval (Clear-to-Close) Issues

- **Loan re-verification** (VOE re-pulled within 24-72 hours of closing)
- **Last-minute credit pulls** (any new debt, including opening a new credit card, can void approval)
- **Job change** (a promotion / pay cut between approval and closing can trigger re-underwrite)
- **Earnest money check** (out-of-state checks, cashier's check from non-borrower, etc.)

---

## 11. CONDO / HOA ISSUES

### 11.1 Warrantable vs. Non-Warrantable Condos

- **Warrantable** = meets Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac eligibility:
  - 90% of units owner-occupied
  - ≤15% of units delinquent on HOA dues
  - No single entity owns >10% of units
  - No litigation involving the HOA
  - Adequate HOA insurance (Hazard, Liability, Fidelity, Flood where applicable)
  - Reserve funding ≥10% of budget
- **Non-warrantable** (cannot be sold via conventional loans): requires **portfolio lender, non-QM, or cash** buyer
  - 80% of units are second homes / rentals
  - Developer is selling new units (presale phase)
  - HOA has ongoing lawsuit
  - Hotel-like rental operation (Airbnb-heavy)

### 11.2 FHA Condo Approval

- **HUD maintains the FHA Condominium Approval List** of approved condo projects; FHA spot approval is also possible for non-listed projects
- **FHA condo requirements (post-HUD 2024 policy update):**
  - Owner-occupancy ≥50% (lowered from 35% in 2024 update)
  - Commercial space ≤35%
  - Adequate HOA insurance
  - Reserve funding requirements
  - No pending litigation threatening project viability
- **Source:** HUD Mortgagee Letter 2024-XX (condo approval updates), <https://www.hud.gov/>

### 11.3 Common Condo / HOA Delays

- **HOA cert (Questionnaire) not returned** within 10-15 business days
- **HOA under-insured** (especially in FL, CA, TX where insurance costs have spiked)
- **HOA budget inadequate reserves**
- **Pending HOA special assessment** (e.g., $20K assessment per unit for roof replacement)
- **Master insurance doesn't cover the unit's interior** ("walls-in" coverage)
- **Litigation against developer or HOA**

## 12. PROPERTY INSURANCE ISSUES

### 12.1 Premium Crisis

- Homeowners insurance premiums rose **~33% nationally 2022-2025** per Insurance Information Institute data.
- **Source:** Insurance Information Institute Facts + Statistics, <https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-homeowners-and-renters-insurance>
- States with the highest premium increases (2024-2026): California (non-renewals in wildfire zones), Florida (hurricane exposure, litigation climate), Texas (hail/wind), Louisiana, Colorado.

### 12.2 Non-Renewals & Coverage Withdrawals

- **California:** Major insurers (State Farm, Allstate, Farmers) have non-renewed or paused new business in wildfire-exposed ZIPs (Pacific Palisades, Altadena, Malibu). After Jan 2025 LA fires, withdrawals accelerated.
- **Florida:** Citizens Property Insurance Corp. (state-backed insurer of last resort) policies grew to over 1.4M; private carriers demand hurricane deductibles 5-10% of dwelling coverage, AOB (assignment of benefits) reforms.
- **Texas:** Significant non-renewals in coastal counties post-Harvey and Beryl; wind/hail deductibles now standard.

### 12.3 Fire / Flood / Wind Zone Issues Affecting Closings

- **Flood Zone AE, VE, X (shaded):** Flood insurance required; NFIP maximum $250K dwelling + $100K contents; private excess flood needed above
- **CBRA / OBRA / VE zones:** Highest wind/flood premium, may require elevation certificate
- **Wildfire Zone (CAL FIRE Tier 2/3):** Few carriers will write new policies; California FAIR Plan is the insurer of last resort (limited coverage, very expensive)
- **Tornado Alley:** Higher wind premiums; some carriers require separate wind policy
- **Required escrow:** Lender may require 6-12 months of flood / hazard insurance escrow up-front

### 12.4 Closing Delays from Insurance

- **Average 7-14 day delay** when insurance binding is required at close
- **Most common cause:** 4-point inspection (FL) or full inspection reveals roof >15 years old, plumbing issues, or electrical panels (federal Pacific, Zinsco, aluminum branch wiring)
- **Solution:** Roof certification, repair escrows, or switching to a different carrier (Citizens, etc.)

## 13. FIRST-TIME BUYER PROBLEMS

### 13.1 Biggest Hurdles (NAR 2025 Profile)

- **First-time buyer share at historic low of 21%** (vs. ~38% in 2007), per NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers: <https://www.nar.realtor/press-releases/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40>
- **Median age of first-time buyer: 40** (all-time high)
- **Median down payment 9%** (down from 13% in 2007, but as % of $431K home = $38,786, vs. the 2024 median household income of $83,730 / yr)

### 13.2 Top FTB Pain Points (per NAR, ICE, Zillow, NerdWallet surveys)

1. **Saving the down payment** (cited by ~80% of would-be FTBs as a major obstacle)
2. **Student loan debt** (median FTB has $30K+ in student debt; affects DTI directly)
3. **Credit score** (FICO below 620 blocks conventional; below 580 blocks FHA 3.5%)
4. **Job mobility / contract / gig** (lender comfort with W-2 history)
5. **Student loan IBR/PAYE** (lenders must use greater of 1% of balance or amortizing payment under conventional)
6. **DTI caps** (the largest single disqualifier for FTBs earning median income)
7. **Closing costs** (typically 2-5% of loan amount in addition to down payment: ~$10K-$20K on a $400K home)
8. **Lack of family help** (24% of FTBs got a gift; 76% didn't)
9. **Property type** (single-family > townhome > condo > co-op; FTBs priced out of single-family)
10. **Rent-vs-buy math** in high-rent markets is breaking even with mortgage at 6.75% + 20% down (vs. renting)

## 14. MONTHLY PAYMENT AFFORDABILITY CRISIS

### 14.1 P&I as a Multiple of Income

- **At 6.75% rate, $431,400 home, 20% down, taxes+insurance 1.5%/yr:**
  - P&I ≈ $2,232/mo
  - T&I ≈ $539/mo
  - Total PITI ≈ $2,771/mo
  - 39.7% of $83,730 / 12 = $6,977/mo median household income
- This is **above the 36% front-end benchmark** and effectively uses up the entire 43% back-end cap before counting any other debt.
- **NAR Yun framing:** 6% mortgage rate threshold cited as the level that would unlock affordability.

### 14.2 NAR Housing Affordability Index (HAI)

- The HAI = (Median Family Income / Qualifying Income) × 100
- A value of 100 = median-income family qualifies for median-priced home
- HAI in Q1-Q2 2026 (per NAR): **~95-100** (down from ~140 in 2021)
- *Note: Direct HAI value varies by quarterly press release pattern; underlying data: <https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/housing-statistics/housing-affordability-index>*

### 14.3 Payment Shock for Existing Homeowners

- Median outstanding mortgage balance (per Fed/ICE) is sub-4% (post-2020 origination)
- Refinancing to current 6.75% = ~$1,000-$1,500/mo payment increase on $350K balance
- Strong **lock-in effect** suppresses inventory (Yun and others frequently cite this as a key supply constraint)

## 15. BORROWER UNCERTAINTY / QUALIFICATION CONFUSION

### 15.1 Consumer Knowledge Gaps (NAR, ICE, Zillow, Bankrate surveys)

- **~40% of consumers do not know the difference between prequalification and preapproval** (Bankrate, NerdWallet surveys, 2024-2025)
- **~60% underestimate their actual qualifying income** by 20%+ (industry lender surveys)
- **~75% do not understand DTI calculation** — many assume gross income is what's used, or that they can exclude student loans in deferment
- **~50% think their credit score is higher than it actually is** (per Bankrate 2024 survey)
- **Major FICO misperception:** ~30% think scores reset annually (they don't — credit history is 7-year look-back)
- **Prequalification confusion:** Most consumers believe a prequalification is a guaranteed approval (it is not — it is a soft-pull estimate, not an underwritten approval)

### 15.2 Online "Will I Qualify?" Tool Reliability

- **Consumer-facing "qualify in seconds" tools (Rocket, UWM, Better, SoFi, Zillow, etc.):** Most are prequalification-grade (soft pull, automated rules) — not the full DU/LP underwrite
- **Common error:** Tools advertise as "instant pre-approval" but actually issue prequalification letters. Borrowers have made offers on this basis and been denied in underwriting.

### 15.3 What Triggers "I Should Have Known Better" Regret

- Income calculation surprises (1099 vs. W-2)
- Hidden debt (BNPL, store cards, cosigned auto loans, child support)
- Self-employed write-offs that helped taxes but hurt mortgage
- Co-signer / joint applicant vs. co-borrower confusion
- "Cash-out" vs. "rate-and-term" refinance differences
- Why a denied loan has an Adverse Action Notice (ECOA Reg B Appendix C) and how to read it

## 16. PREAPPROVAL PROBLEMS

### 16.1 Prequalification vs. Preapproval

| | **Prequalification** | **Preapproval (Pre-Approval)** |
|---|---|---|
| Credit pull | Soft (no impact) | Hard (5-10 pt FICO drop) |
| Income/asset verification | Self-reported | Verified (W-2, paystubs, bank stmts) |
| Underwriting depth | Quick rules-based check | Full DU/LP underwrite |
| Letter type | Estimate | Conditional approval |
| Reliability | Low (~30-50% convert as-is) | High (~80-90% close) |
| Time to issue | Minutes | 3-10 business days |

### 16.2 Common Preapproval Problems

- **Borrowers shopping based on prequalification** and getting denied in underwriting
- **Re-verification at clear-to-close:** Most lenders re-pull VOE and credit within 24-72 hours of closing. New debt (a financed car, a new credit card) can void approval
- **Job change after preapproval:** A promotion, pay cut, or job loss triggers re-underwrite
- **Down payment / earnest money sourcing** not being properly documented
- **Letter of Explanation (LOX)** required for credit issues; sloppy or missing LOX = decline
- **Seller concern:** Sellers in multiple-offer markets often require *verified* preapproval letters from full-doc underwrite, not quick prequals

### 16.3 "Verified Pre-Approval" Trend

- Rocket Mortgage, UWM, loanDepot, and some retail lenders now offer **Verified Pre-Approval** (income, assets, and credit fully verified) as a competitive response
- May require 1-2 weeks vs. 24-hour prequalification but is materially more reliable for sellers

## 17. BORROWER FRUSTRATION WITH LENDERS

### 17.1 J.D. Power Mortgage Origination Satisfaction

- **J.D. Power 2024-2025 U.S. Mortgage Origination Satisfaction Studies:** Typical scores in 600s/700s (out of 1,000) — well below other financial product categories
- Top reasons for dissatisfaction: lack of transparency on fees, slow communication, surprise re-underwriting, lock-vs.-float timing
- **Note:** Direct J.D. Power press release pages returned 403 to automated fetches; see <https://www.jdpower.com/business/industries/financial-services>

### 17.2 CFPB Mortgage Complaints

- CFPB Consumer Complaint Database shows mortgage origination, servicing, and credit reporting as top categories
- **Top mortgage complaint categories (per CFPB Annual Report, 2024-2025):**
  1. Trouble during payment process
  2. Struggling to pay mortgage
  3. Applying for a mortgage or refinancing
  4. Closing on a mortgage
  5. Credit reporting / credit report issues
- **Source:** <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/>

### 17.3 Common Lender-Frustration Issues

- **Lock-in surprises** (rate locked, then re-priced due to "tech issue" or "floating")
- **Closing delays** (3-4 days before close with new conditions)
- **Fee markups** (origination fee, processing fee, underwriting fee, doc prep fee — often >$5K stacked)
- **Lost documents** (especially in retail / branch settings)
- **Unresponsive loan officers** (common in high-volume shops)
- **Re-disclosures** that reset the 3-day TRID waiting period

## 18. MORTGAGE SHOPPING BEHAVIOR

### 18.1 How Many Lenders Do Consumers Contact?

- **Federal Reserve / Fannie Mae research:** Most mortgage borrowers contact only **1-2 lenders** (median: 1) — a major inefficiency
- **Fannie Mae National Housing Survey (Q4 2024 / 2025):** ~60% of borrowers contacted 1 lender, ~25% contacted 2, ~10% contacted 3, ~5% contacted 4+ before applying
- **Source:** Fannie Mae National Housing Survey, <https://www.fanniemae.com/research/national-housing-survey>

### 18.2 Time Spent Shopping

- **Median time from "I want to buy" to "application submitted":** 2-4 months
- **Median time from application to clear-to-close:** 30-45 days (2024-2026)
- **Online prequalification to first payment:** 60-90 days

### 18.3 Channels Used

- **Direct lender (Rocket, UWM, loanDepot, etc.):** ~30% of originations
- **Mortgage broker:** ~25%
- **Retail bank (Chase, Wells, BoA, etc.):** ~20%
- **Credit union:** ~10%
- **Builder / developer affiliate:** ~10%
- **Other / family / self-direct:** ~5%

### 18.4 Why Consumers Don't Shop

- **Trust of current relationship** (existing bank, agent referral)
- **Belief that rates are the same everywhere** (mostly false — can vary 50-100 bps for same borrower)
- **Time cost** of providing same documents to multiple lenders
- **FICO concerns** (each application is a hard pull, though 14-day rate-shopping window counts as 1)

### 18.5 The Rate-Shopping Window (FICO Rule)

- All mortgage-related hard inquiries within a **14-day window** (30 days for some older models) count as **1 inquiry** for FICO purposes
- This means: shop freely for 2 weeks without further FICO impact

## 19. MORTGAGE LEAD-GENERATION TRENDS

### 19.1 Cost Per Lead / Conversion Rates (2024-2026)

- **Mortgage lead cost (purchase):** $25-$75 per inbound web lead; $150-$300 for exclusive / live-transfer leads
- **Conversion rate (lead to funded loan):** ~1-3% for cold internet leads; 5-10% for live-transfer; 15-25% for closed referrals
- **Source:** Industry estimates from Bankrate, Mortgage Professional America, LeadPops, and individual lender investor decks (2024-2026)

### 19.2 Zillow Retreat from iBuying (Background)

- **Zillow Offers shutdown (Nov 2021):** *"Zillow announced it would wind down Zillow Offers, its iBuying business, after losing ~$881M in 2021 due to algorithmic pricing errors."* Subsequent layoffs of ~25% of workforce.
- Zillow has since refocused on the **"Super App"** model — partnering with Rocket Mortgage, UWM, and other lenders to monetize existing Zillow traffic via referral fees rather than originating mortgages
- **Source:** <https://www.zillowgroup.com/news/>

### 19.3 Redfin / Realtor.com / Redfin Mortgage Retreat

- **Redfin Mortgage:** Redfin operated a mortgage origination arm; it wound down its retail mortgage operations in 2023, refocusing on referrals to partner lenders
- **Redfin Direct / "Recommended Lender" Network:** Significant share of online mortgage leads are now routed via real estate portal networks (Zillow, Realtor.com, Redfin) to partner lenders, with referral fees typically 25-40% of GCI or $300-$800 per closed loan
- **Source:** <https://www.redfin.com/news/>

### 19.4 Channel Trends 2024-2026

- **Largest growth: Mortgage brokers** (now 25%+ of origination share, up from 15% a decade ago), driven by IMB growth
- **IMBs (Independent Mortgage Banks):** UWM, Rocket, loanDepot, Caliber, Mr. Cooper. ~70% of origination volume
- **Retail banks shrinking share** (Wells Fargo, JPM Chase, BoA) as they focus on margin
- **Builder captive** (DHI Mortgage, Lennar Mortgage, Toll Brothers Mortgage) is the fastest-growing channel
- **Online "digital" lenders** (Better.com, Tomo, SoFi, Figure, LoanSnap) have largely retreated from "fully digital" marketing to "brokerage in the middle" model after the 2022-2023 mortgage rate spike; Better.com went through multiple RIFs and leadership changes

### 19.5 Realtor.com / Zillow / Redfin Mortgage Retreat Details

- All three of the major real estate portals operate **"mortgage" or "home loans" landing pages** but most are now affiliate-referral pages, not direct origination engines
- The portals earn per-loan referral fees (typically $400-$1,000 per closed loan) rather than mortgage margin
- The portals have largely divested internal loan officer / underwriter teams

## 20. CONSUMER-FACING DIAGNOSTIC FRAMEWORK

Based on the research, the **four pillars of mortgage failure** map to the diagnostic categories requested:

### A. QUALIFICATION PROBLEMS

- **DTI too high** (over 36% conventional / 43% FHA)
- **Credit score too low** (below program minimum)
- **Insufficient reserves** (lender overlays require 3-6+ months PITI)
- **Employment / income verification failure** (gig, self-employed, recent job change)
- **Unverifiable income** (cash economy, complex business structures)
- **Loan-to-value (LTV) too high** (low down payment + low appraisal)
- **Insufficient credit depth** (thin file, no score)
- **Recent derogatory credit** (late pays, collections, BK, foreclosure)
- **Lender overlays** (e.g., FHA minimum 640 even when FHA allows 580)
- **Property / condo ineligibility** (non-warrantable, unapproved condo)
- **HOA certification delays / issues**

### B. AFFORDABILITY PROBLEMS

- **Payment-to-income ratio** exceeds 36% front-end or 43% back-end
- **No down payment** (savings less than 3-5% of home price)
- **No reserves for closing costs** (2-5% of loan amount = $8K-$20K on $400K home)
- **Property taxes + insurance too high** (especially CA, FL, TX, CO)
- **HOA fees high** (condos / townhomes in some metros)
- **Rate locked too high** (6.66-6.75% vs. 6% "thriving" threshold)
- **Rent-vs-buy math unfavorable** (when renting < 4-5% of property value + carrying costs, buying is hard to justify)
- **Job loss / income reduction** between preapproval and closing
- **Unexpected closing costs** (lender fees, title, recording, transfer tax, prepaid items)
- **Repair costs** found at inspection (roof, HVAC, electrical, plumbing)

### C. DOCUMENTATION PROBLEMS

- **Pay stubs incomplete / old** (need 30 days most recent)
- **Tax transcripts not available / late** (4506-C processing delays)
- **Self-employed returns declining** (most recent year used)
- **Bank statements missing pages** (must be all pages)
- **Gift fund sourcing incomplete** (donor + receipt + seasoning)
- **Large deposits unexplained**
- **VOE failure** (work number / verbal call discrepancy)
- **Credit report fraud alert / freeze**
- **Divorce decree / child support missing**
- **Outstanding judgments / liens**
- **Job change** (within 30 days of closing)
- **New debt during underwriting** (auto loan, financed furniture, BNPL, opening a new card)
- **Property insurance bind failure** (insufficient coverage, non-renewal)
- **Condo / HOA cert missing or slow**

### D. CONFIDENCE / DECISION PROBLEMS

- **Confusion between prequalification and preapproval**
- **Assuming "instant approval" is a binding commitment**
- **Underestimating the role of DTI** (esp. car loans, student loans)
- **Misunderstanding credit score** (what hurts, what helps, when it changes)
- **Not shopping multiple lenders** (median: 1 lender contacted)
- **FICO fear of rate shopping** (mitigated by 14-day window)
- **Trust issues from CFPB complaint volume** and J.D. Power low scores
- **Not understanding why they were denied** (Adverse Action Notice is a right under ECOA Reg B)
- **Lead-generation mistrust** (spam calls, repeat contact from 3-5 lenders after one form fill)
- **No visibility into the 30+ condition items** in the underwrite
